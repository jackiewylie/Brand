Concept

Before the open.
You already know.

First Light names the moment the product exists for: 6:12 AM, pre-market, the first signal of the morning. The brand is temporal advantage — the discipline of knowing before others, documented before anyone checks. The visual language is the gradient from dark to first light: a deep blue-black that bleeds into the warmth of an early morning horizon.

Where Territories A, B, and C argue from what the product IS, Territory D argues from when the product matters. "Before the open, not accelerate your workflow" — the PM audience's forbidden phrase becomes the brand's claim. The pre-dawn metaphor is not decorative: it is the precise emotional state of an analyst who needs to be ready at the market open and knows they are, because the system was watching all night.

The Horizon — Territory D's Signature Visual Element

The gradient horizon — dark blue fading to the first warmth of morning — is the brand's central visual element. The Flare orange node appears at the horizon line: the first event of the morning, the moment something material registered while everyone was still asleep.

06:14 ET
Material event detected · 3 signals before the open

The node on the horizon is the Step logomark in its most literal form: a point where the line registers an event. The gradient is ambient on brand surfaces — a slow shift that communicates the passing of night — and is still on product UI surfaces (motion constraint: ambient on brand, static in product). The horizon can be a thin horizontal rule across the top of any surface, with the node marking the most recent event.

Color System

Deep blue-black base with a warm golden horizon accent. Flare orange at the first-event node. The palette carries the pre-dawn gradient — cold navy fading to warm amber at the edge. This is the most emotionally differentiated palette of any territory, and the furthest from the neutral institutional registers of competitors.

Bloomberg differentiation: Bloomberg Terminal uses warm amber on near-black — a terminal aesthetic built for all-day use. First Light uses cold blue-black fading to warm gold — a temporal aesthetic built for the pre-dawn moment. The warmth arrives at the edge (the horizon), not as persistent interface chrome. The shift from cold base to warm horizon is the brand's motion in time, not Bloomberg's static amber grid.
Color constraint note (D7 compliance): The dawn amber (#C4A560) is in the yellow-gold range. Phase 1 D7 notes that the v1 identity yellow was "retired" — the v2 identity replaced it with Flare orange. Territory D's dawn amber is not yellow in the sense of the retired v1 accent; it is a warm neutralized gold used as a gradient endpoint, not as a signal or interactive color. The Phase 4 color decision must confirm whether this reintroduction of warm gold is within the D7 constraint, or whether it requires an amendment to D7.
Competitor swap test — full six-competitor panel:

FM-04 Resolution — Editorial Identity Institutional Compatibility
VERDICT: CONDITIONAL — Phase 4 test required before commitment

Territory D is the only territory where FM-04 is a live risk rather than a cleared gate. The reason: Instrument Serif italic appears on professional brand surfaces. This is the editorial identity question that FM-04 specifically flags.

The Phase 4 FM-04 test must cover three distinct institutional context types — each represents a materially different gatekeeper with different aesthetic expectations and different tolerance for editorial-register typography:

Institutional contextTest questionFriction level
Bank procurement portalDoes Instrument Serif italic on a product page read as credible-professional to a bank's technology procurement team, or does it read as editorial/consumer?Medium — banks maintain conservative aesthetic standards for vendor materials; modern premium typography is accepted in financial products but must not read as a consumer brand
Pension fund LP due diligence questionnaireDoes Instrument Serif on a DDQ cover page and section headers communicate institutional seriousness to a pension fund's manager selection team?High — pension fund LPs are the most conservative institutional capital; any aesthetic that suggests editorial or consumer brand may trigger questions about product maturity and intended audience
SEC-registered investment adviser marketing reviewDoes Instrument Serif pass a compliance officer's standard for professional presentation in materials distributed to institutional clients under the Investment Advisers Act?Low-Medium — RIA compliance review focuses primarily on substantive claims and disclosures; aesthetic professionalism is a factor in firm selection but typography is less likely to be flagged than in LP due diligence

Passing criteria: The FM-04 test passes when a panel of ≥5 institutional professionals — at least one from each of the three context types above — rates Instrument Serif as "credible professional" (not "editorial or consumer-facing") in a blind comparison against Territories A and B using identical content templates. Threshold: ≥4/5 panelists must rate Instrument Serif as credible-professional across all three context types. If the threshold is not met in any context type, Territory D advances with Schibsted Grotesk as the display face replacement.

Two possible outcomes of the Phase 4 FM-04 test:

Test outcomeImplication
Instrument Serif passes all three context typesTerritory D advances with Instrument Serif on brand surfaces. The editorial-to-professional register question is resolved in favor of the serif's adaptability. This would be the strongest brand continuity outcome — maximum visual connection between consumer Lede and professional brand.
Instrument Serif fails one or more context typesTerritory D requires modification: Schibsted Grotesk replaces Instrument Serif as the display face. The gradient horizon and temporal concept survive; only the typography changes. The result is a brand that argues from temporal advantage in a sans-serif register — still distinctive, but with lower consumer-brand continuity. This is the conditional advancement path.
The gauntlet position: Territory D cannot be selected by Phase 3 without explicitly accepting the FM-04 conditional. A Phase 3 selection of Territory D must include: "this selection is conditional on FM-04 passing in Phase 4 across bank procurement, pension fund DDQ, and RIA marketing review contexts; if Instrument Serif fails the ≥4/5 threshold in any context type, Phase 4 replaces it with Schibsted Grotesk at the display role." The territory concept (temporal, gradient, pre-dawn) is not dependent on the serif; the serif is a bonus that may or may not survive institutional testing.
Naming Compatibility

First Light's naming needs a word that carries temporal weight and orientation — something about positioning at a specific moment, before everything else.

Bearing
Strongest fit
A bearing is a navigational reading taken at a specific moment to establish your position. "Get your Bearing before the open." "The Morning Bearing." The temporal claim and the navigation metaphor are the same claim: you know where you stand because the platform took your position in the pre-dawn window. "Bearing by Lede" in the italic serif is visually coherent — the navigation instrument rendered in editorial type.
Brief
Good fit
A Brief is a daily intelligence document — the pre-dawn summary. "Your morning Brief" = the First Light deliverable named exactly. "Brief by Lede" in Instrument Serif italic is clean. The intelligence brief connotation pairs well with the temporal-moment brand concept, though the word itself doesn't carry the navigational precision of Bearing.
Vigil
Adequate fit
A vigil is a sustained watch through the night — monitoring until something happens. This is the product concept (continuous monitoring), but the temporal metaphor doesn't align with the brand moment (the detection, not the watching). "Vigil by Lede" in the serif is plausible. The word describes the process, not the event. Works; not the strongest pairing for Territory D.
