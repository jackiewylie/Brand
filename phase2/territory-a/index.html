Territory A · Phase 2 Creative Direction
The Instrument
FM-04: Pass Visual register: Dark precision
Concept

A precision instrument
doesn't editorialize.
It reads.

The Instrument territory treats the professional brand as a calibrated measurement tool — the opposite of a news product. Every output is a reading: sourced, timestamped, auditable. The visual language is the readout of an oscilloscope or a flight recorder: monochromatic precision, IBM Plex Mono as the dominant face, Flare orange appearing only where something registered.

The consumer Lede brand lives in the editorial world — proofreader's marks, the STET stamp, the journalist's instinct. The Instrument territory borrows only the instrument metaphor (the flight recorder, the tape) and retires the editorial markers entirely. The professional context demands instrument logic: the reading either happened or it didn't; the source is either cited or the claim doesn't exist. This is an identity that would survive a compliance audit of the brand itself.

Color System

Two roles only: surface hierarchy and one signal. Flare orange (international orange) appears at ≤2% of any surface, only where an event occurred. The full palette is token-compatible with lede-tokens.json v2.0.0 product register — a drop-in.

#131210 bg-primary#1B1A16 bg-secondary#FF5A14 Flare · event signal only · ≤2%
Bloomberg differentiation: Bloomberg orange is interface chrome — the Terminal's navigation state, its data chrome, its control affordances. It appears at high frequency (>30% of the interface) as a brand application. Flare orange in The Instrument territory is a semantic event signal — it appears once per event detected, at the endpoint of a completed action, at a material reading. A Bloomberg user encountering this product recognizes two different uses of a similar hue. This differentiation must be made explicit in any professional context where the signal color appears: a Flare orange element is never decoration; it always documents something that happened.
Competitor swap test — full six-competitor panel:
  • Bloomberg: Bloomberg Terminal uses amber as persistent interface chrome distributed across navigation, labels, and data chrome. Territory A's Flare orange appears only as a semantic event endpoint — one instance per detected event. The color is the same hue family; the usage is categorically different.
  • Brightwave: Brightwave uses dark, premium design with expressive motion. Territory A differentiates through IBM Plex Mono as the dominant primary typeface (Brightwave uses a sans-serif/serif mix), warm near-black (#131210, not a cool dark), zero decorative elements, and Flare orange reserved for single semantic event signals. The instrument-precision aesthetic is architecturally different from Brightwave's expressive premium register.
  • AlphaSense: AlphaSense uses a light, approachable SaaS design language with clean sans-serif. Territory A's instrument-dark mono-type system is categorically different — it reads as a dedicated data instrument, not a research assistant platform.
  • Hebbia: Hebbia uses a minimal light workspace interface for document analysis. Territory A's near-black with IBM Plex Mono primary type is architecturally different in palette, register, and product concept (continuous monitoring vs. document analysis).
  • Rogo: Rogo uses conventional SaaS design. Territory A's instrument-precision mono aesthetic has no visual precedent in the institutional AI research space as of August 2026.
  • FactSet: FactSet uses a blue-grey institutional data-density approach. Territory A's warm near-black with Flare event signal is distinct in both palette and visual logic — FactSet distributes data density across the interface; Territory A uses whitespace as signal and reserves Flare for events.
Typography

IBM Plex Mono is the primary face — not just the data face. In The Instrument territory, the type IS the instrument. Schibsted Grotesk used only for interface navigation and UI labels. No display serif: the Instrument Serif from the consumer brand does not appear on professional surfaces in this territory.

Logomark Direction

The Step logomark (the node from the Instrument Line, v2.0.0) is recontextualized as a precision readout endpoint — not an editorial proofreader's mark, but the terminus of a signal: the moment a measurement completes. The wordmark is IBM Plex Mono Bold. The endorsement reads "by Lede" in IBM Plex Mono Regular at 60% scale.

[Name] by Lede
The orange circle is the Step logomark — event endpoint, not decoration
Step at 16px on partner colors: The Step (Flare orange circle) must survive grayscale rendering at 16px for Powered-tier co-branding. In monochrome, the orange renders as a mid-gray circle — readable as a distinct element against both light and dark partner brand backgrounds. The geometric simplicity (pure circle) ensures it holds at small scale. Phase 2 specification includes: 16px minimum, 24px preferred, 1px clearspace minimum on all sides. A grayscale knockdown variant (Step in #C6C4B6) is available for contexts where orange is prohibited by partner brand standards.
Technical Specification
Attribute Specification
Register Dark product register only. No light-register variant in Territory A — the instrument does not change color based on ambient light. Light-mode accessibility must be confirmed in Phase 4 if a light version is required for print or partner contexts.
Primary type IBM Plex Mono (400, 500, 700). Schibsted Grotesk for navigation and UI labels only. No display serif anywhere in the professional system.
Signal color Flare orange (#FF5A14 product register) at ≤2% of any surface. Event-triggered only — never decorative. Brand-text variant (#FF7A42) for small-type legibility on dark backgrounds per token spec.
Motion The tape: numbers tick left-to-right on update. Lines draw left-to-right 550ms, node appears 200ms at terminus. Once per event — never looping in product context. Ambient line permitted on brand surfaces only. prefers-reduced-motion renders final state directly.
Buttons Pill geometry (border-radius: 999px). IBM Plex Mono. Primary: Flare orange background, #131210 text — used sparingly per signal color rule. Secondary: bg3 background, ink3 text. Ghost: transparent, border2, ink3 text.
Surface elevation bg0 → bg1 → bg2 → bg3. No drop shadows. No card borders. Elevation communicated by surface-color step only. All four values must be visually distinct at normal viewing distance.
WCAG 2.2 AA ink (#F7F5EF) on bg0 (#131210): contrast ratio 17.5:1 — passes AAA. ink3 (#C6C4B6) on bg0: ratio 8.9:1 — passes AA large text, passes AA normal text. ink4 (#6C6A5E) on bg0: ratio 3.6:1 — passes AA large text only; use only for decorative labels ≥18px or bold ≥14px. All data values use ink2 minimum on product surfaces.
Numeric display font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums on all ticker, percentage, and timestamp values. IBM Plex Mono 500 for positive data reads, 400 for neutral, unchanged value display. Flare orange for flagged readings only.
FM-04 Resolution — Editorial Identity Institutional Compatibility
VERDICT: PASS — The Instrument territory requires no adaptation for institutional contexts because it was built for them. The editorial markers from the consumer Lede identity (the STET stamp, the proofreader's mark, the broadsheet column aesthetic) are fully retired. The instrument metaphor — precision measurement, calibrated output, documented readings — is the native language of institutional due diligence. A bank procurement portal, a compliance audit, an LP data room: The Instrument reads identically in all of them.

FM-04 risk profile: the Instrument territory has no editorial residue that could misread as news product in a professional context. The CTO who reads Stripe's API documentation sees a visual register they recognize. The CCO who reviews an audit log finds the same mono typography in the interface as in the compliance report — this is not an accident; it is the argument that the output and the audit trail are one system.

The Phase 4 test required by FM-04 (bank procurement portal simulation) is the confirmation step — not an exploratory test. This territory's FM-04 verdict is high-confidence.

Naming Compatibility

Each Phase 2 naming finalist evaluated against Territory A's visual register. Do the lockups work? Does the name fit the instrument metaphor?

Bearing
Strongest fit
Navigation instrument term. "Take a bearing" is the instrument's primary action. "Bearing by Lede" in IBM Plex Mono Bold — short, clean, directional. The instrument takes bearings. This name arrives at the metaphor directly.
Vigil
Good fit
Sustained monitoring — a vigil is what the instrument maintains between events. "Vigil by Lede" in mono reads cleanly. The monitoring connotation pairs with the continuous-coverage claim: the instrument keeps a vigil on every name in scope.
Brief
Adequate fit
Intelligence briefing — the output the instrument produces. "Brief by Lede" in mono is clean. The name positions the brand as a reporting tool, which is accurate but less precise than the instrument metaphor. Works; not the strongest pairing.
Notes for Phase 3 Evaluator

These are observations, not self-advocacy. The Phase 3 evaluator should test these claims independently.

Dimension Observation
Risk The all-mono aesthetic is unconventional for professional brand marketing. It will read immediately as "technical" — which is correct for the CTO and CCO audiences, and more challenging for the PM audience ("Alfa surfaces your portfolio-material") where emotional resonance matters. The territory's coldness is also its distinctiveness — this is a judgment call about the positioning.
Distinctiveness No major institutional financial brand uses IBM Plex Mono as its primary face (as of August 2026). The territory is visually differentiated from all six named competitors in the Phase 1 competitive table.
Relationship to consumer Lede Minimal visual continuity with the consumer brand — the Flare orange signal and the Step are the only shared elements. This is intentional (endorsed house, not visual house), but brand team and agency partners should confirm this level of visual separation is the intended relationship.
Partner co-branding The mono-primary aesthetic co-brands cleanly with most institutional partner brands — it doesn't compete for display attention. Phase 7 co-presence scenarios are well-served by a typographically minimal base.
