Naming Funnel — Three Finalists

Phase 2 produces three post-Alfa professional product name finalists meeting all seven D5 criteria. Phase 3 blind gauntlet selects one. All three are unencumbered by existing professional-brand-gauntlet commitments — none has been recommended or pre-selected. The Phase 3 evaluator chooses blind.

D5 criteria reminder (from Phase 1): (1) distinct from LedeAI in class 42; (2) legible to US institutional buyers without explanation; (3) no sanctions or controversial-entity association; (4) entity-clean in class 36 and class 42 (requires legal vetting — all candidates flagged as conditional on counsel); (5) carries the professional intelligence/coverage promise; (6) works as master brand for all five tiers; (7) domain available (.com or .ai preferred).
Standfirst
Finalist #1 · Bench candidate #1 (red-team document)

The standfirst is the introductory paragraph that follows a headline in quality journalism — a brief, dense summary that captures the story's essence before the full account. In professional investment intelligence: the standfirst is what arrived before the market opened, the ranked-material read that precedes everything else. It is the product's action in a word.

Pronunciation
STAND-first (two syllables)
Meaning tax
UK newsroom jargon. "Bury the lede" is common American idiom; "standfirst" is not. US professional buyers may not recognize the term — requires the "by Lede" endorsement to carry context.
Product lexicon
Weak. "A standfirst" means nothing to US users outside media. The morning brief can be "your standfirst" but it requires training.
Distinct from LedeAI
Strong — different word, different class
Institutional legibility
Moderate — UK term unfamiliar to US buyers
Sanctions risk
None identified
D5 criteria
Meets 5/7 — legibility and lexicon are the gaps
Master brand test: Standfirst Pro / Standfirst Prime / Standfirst Platform / Standfirst Powered / Standfirst Powered+ — all clear. "Standfirst by Lede" — clear. Territory fit: strongest in Territory D (temporal, editorial serif); strong in Territory B (document, formal). Conditional: trademark clearance in class 36 and class 42 required before use.
Bellwether
Finalist #2 · Bench candidate #2 (red-team document)

The bellwether is the stock that signals the market's direction — the investment community's native term for the first indicator. It is the analyst's word, used in earnings calls and research notes, familiar without explanation to every buy-side professional. The product monitors the bellwether readings in every name before anyone else does.

Pronunciation
BELL-weth-er (three syllables)
Spelling tax
Chronic misspelling as "bellweather" — the animal origin ("wether" = castrated male sheep) is invisible to most users, making the correct spelling counterintuitive. Larger than Standfirst's pronunciation tax because users type it.
Product lexicon
None. "A bellwether" = the stock that signals the market, not the product's output. The product can't produce "a bellwether" — the word means the stock, not the alert.
Distinct from LedeAI
Strong — completely different register
Institutional legibility
Strong — finance-native term, no explanation needed
Sanctions risk
None identified
D5 criteria
Meets 5/7 — lexicon and spelling are the gaps
Master brand test: Bellwether Pro / Bellwether Prime / Bellwether Platform / Bellwether Powered / Bellwether Powered+ — all clear (three-syllable master brand reads long in lockups). "Bellwether by Lede" — clear, professional. Territory fit: strongest in Territory A (instrument) and C (matrix — flagged cell = bellwether stock); good in B and D. Conditional: trademark clearance in class 36 and class 42 required; domain availability (.com likely taken; .ai to verify).
Bearing
Finalist #3 · New candidate from Phase 2 naming work

A bearing is a navigator's term for one's directional orientation — the precise angle between a reference point and a target. "Get your bearing" = determine your position and direction. For a professional investor: Bearing surfaces the orientation reading on what matters in your portfolio before you ask for it. The word is short, common, institutional, and carries both "orientation" and "enduring" (to bear witness) as secondary meanings.

Pronunciation
BEAR-ing (two syllables). No ambiguity.
Taxes
Primary "bearing" meanings: (1) navigation direction — intended; (2) mechanical bearing (ball bearing) — industrial connotation; (3) "bearing" as comportment — archaic. The mechanical association is the only tax; in a professional financial context, the navigation meaning dominates.
Product lexicon
Strong. "Get your Bearing" = orientation briefing. "A Bearing on TSLA" = the platform's coverage reading on a name. "The Morning Bearing" = the pre-market briefing product name. Builds naturally.
Distinct from LedeAI
Strong — completely different class and metaphor
Institutional legibility
Strong — common English word, universally legible
Sanctions risk
None identified
D5 criteria
Meets 6/7 — trademark clearance pending
Master brand test: Bearing Pro / Bearing Prime / Bearing Platform / Bearing Powered / Bearing Powered+ — all clear. Short master brand works well in all lockup sizes. "Bearing by Lede" — clean, professional, directional. Territory fit: strongest in Territory A (instrument navigation) and Territory C (bearing as the grid's output). Conditional: trademark clearance in class 36 and class 42 required before use. Domain: bearing.com likely taken; bearing.ai or getbearing.com to verify. This is the strongest D5 scorer among the three finalists on the criteria that are already checkable.
Naming funnel note for Phase 3 evaluator: All three finalists are conditional on trademark clearance. None has been cleared. If cleared with high opposition risk, the bench activates (Standfirst and Bellwether for each other; a clear alternative to both is required if all three fail — a Phase 3 contingency plan, not a Phase 2 deliverable).

Phase 2 → Phase 3 Handoff

What Phase 3 receives as confirmed inputs vs. what it decides.

Confirmed — Phase 3 inherits as inputs
  • Four complete creative territories (A, B, C, D), each with visual specification, FM-04 verdict, Bloomberg answer, and logomark direction
  • Three naming finalists (Standfirst, Bellwether, Bearing), each evaluated against D5 criteria with explicit condition notes
  • FM-04 clear on A, B, C. D is conditional on Phase 4 test.
  • Territory D carries two open conditions: FM-04 test and D7 amendment for dawn amber
  • All Phase 1 decisions (D1–D7) remain confirmed — Phase 3 does not relitigate them
  • The Powered/Powered+ naming syntax three-way fork (Phase 1 issue #2) is a Phase 3 decision — see naming funnel format below
Phase 3 decides
  • One creative territory selected — from the four, blind to Phase 2 recommendation. The selection is the Phase 3 deliverable. If Territory D is selected, the selection must include explicit acceptance of FM-04 conditional.
  • One name finalist selected — from the three, subject to trademark clearance. The selection is provisional until counsel clears it.
  • Powered/Powered+ attribution syntax — the three-way fork ("Powered by [Name]" / "Powered by [New Name]" / "Powered by Lede") requires a decision rule for which formulation a partner uses when. Phase 3 must make this decision or explicitly defer it to Phase 4 with a stated reason.
  • Transition-period co-presence answer — Phase 3 confirms which territory's direction handles the Boosted.ai + Alfa + Lede simultaneous-in-market scenario, and whether the selected territory requires a Phase 4 amendment to the co-presence rules

Phase 3 Blind Selection Format

The Phase 3 evaluator reviews the four territory pages in isolation — each page is designed in its own aesthetic, so the evaluation is visual as well as strategic. The format for Phase 3 output:

  • Selected territory (A / B / C / D) with explicit rationale against Phase 1 D1–D7 decisions
  • Selected name finalist (Standfirst / Bellwether / Bearing) with explicit rationale against D5 criteria
  • If Territory D selected: explicit acceptance of FM-04 conditional and D7 amendment requirement
  • Powered/Powered+ syntax decision: which formulation, and when
  • Phase 3→4 handoff: one page of inputs Phase 4 inherits as confirmed
