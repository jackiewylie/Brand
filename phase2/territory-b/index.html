Territory B · Phase 2 Creative Direction
The Record
FM-04: Pass Visual register: Light provenance
Concept

Every reading has a provenance.
The record proves it.

The Record treats the professional brand as institutional documentation — not a product, not a platform, but a body of evidence. The visual language is the official document: warm paper ground, Newsreader body type, compressed sans headings, navy rule. Every output looks like something a compliance officer would sign. Because it is.

The boldest claim in the Phase 1 positioning is "verifiable by design." The Record territory makes that claim the visual argument: if the brand looks like an auditable document, it is making the trust case before the copy begins. The aesthetic is deliberately counter-intuitive for a technology brand — closer to a legal brief than a product landing page — which is exactly why it differentiates. Institutional buyers recognize the document register as credible. No competitor occupies it.

Color System

Brand register (light). The primary hue is warm paper — not white, not gray, but the specific off-white of an archival document. Navy as the single structural accent. Flare orange retired from the brand register; it appears only in product UI (data signals) and is not used as a brand color in the document aesthetic.

Bloomberg differentiation: Bloomberg Terminal is a dark interface — the near-black with amber/orange chrome is the institutional terminal aesthetic. The Record territory occupies the opposite register: light, archival, document-like. A Bloomberg Terminal printout and a brief generated by The Record territory are visually distinct from 15 feet. The brand does not compete in the terminal's visual register — it competes in the filing cabinet. This is a fundamental register difference, not a hue difference.
Flare orange in The Record: Flare orange does not appear as a brand color in this territory. It is reserved for product UI (data event signals in the dark product register). On brand-register surfaces (marketing, documents, presentations), the structural color is navy. This is the most conservative interpretation of the Phase 1 color constraint — appearance-earned orange means zero appearances on static brand documents.
Competitor swap test — full six-competitor panel:
  • Bloomberg: Bloomberg Terminal is dark — near-black with amber chrome. The Record occupies the opposite register: light, archival, paper-like. A Bloomberg Terminal printout and a Record-territory intelligence brief are visually distinct at 15 feet. Complete register difference.
  • Brightwave: Brightwave uses dark, expressive, premium design. The Record is light, document-formal, archival. No visual proximity risk — these are opposite ends of the dark/light spectrum.
  • AlphaSense: AlphaSense uses a light SaaS aesthetic with modern sans-serif and research-platform visual language. The Record differentiates through document-aesthetic formality — the filing cabinet, not the research assistant. Newsreader body type and the navy structural accent produce a distinctly archival register that AlphaSense's platform design doesn't occupy.
  • Hebbia: Hebbia's document-analysis workspace is light and modern but functionally focused. The Record's deliberate archival aesthetic (warm paper, document header hierarchy, filing taxonomy) occupies a different register — institutional archive vs. modern workspace.
  • Rogo: Rogo uses conventional institutional SaaS design. The Record's document aesthetic has no parallel in the institutional AI research market as of August 2026.
  • FactSet: FactSet uses blue-grey institutional data density. The Record's warm-paper document aesthetic, navy structural accent, and serif body type are categorically different from FactSet's terminal-adjacent design language.
Token compliance note — proposed brand register amendment: Territory B uses warm-paper surface values that differ from the lede-tokens.json v2.0.0 brand register. Comparison: v2.0.0 specifies surface-primary #F7F5EF / surface-secondary #EFECE2 / surface-tertiary #E6E3D5 / surface-hover #DEDACA. Territory B uses bg0 #F4F2E8 / bg1 #EDE9DA / bg2 #E4E0CE / bg3 #D8D3BE. These deviations shift the brand register slightly warmer and darker, consistent with the archival paper aesthetic of The Record territory. This is an intentional creative choice requiring Phase 4 authorization. If Territory B is selected, Phase 4 must either: (a) align Territory B's surface values to v2.0.0 brand register exactly, or (b) formally amend the brand register token values for the professional brand surface system. Neither option changes the territory's core visual direction — the document aesthetic survives either value set.
Typography

Newsreader is the primary body face — a reading-optimized serif designed for long-form comprehension. Compressed Schibsted Grotesk (Bold, All-Caps, tracked) for structural headings and section labels. IBM Plex Mono for all data values. The combination reads as: a document that was designed to be read carefully, not skimmed.

Logomark Direction

The Step logomark is recontextualized as a document section mark — the break between entries in an official record, the pagination node in a legal brief. The wordmark uses a serif baseline with a compressed sans endorsement line. The lockup is deliberately document-like: not a startup bug, a filing stamp.

FM-04 Resolution — Editorial Identity Institutional Compatibility
VERDICT: PASS (highest confidence of any territory) — The Record territory has no editorial identity at all. It is a document identity — closer to a law firm's filing system than to a newsroom. The Instrument Serif, the STET stamp, the broadsheet column aesthetic from the consumer Lede brand are entirely absent. The brand register that procurement committees, compliance officers, and legal teams encounter is warm paper with navy structure — the native visual language of institutional documentation. There is no FM-04 risk to test; the territory was designed from the institutional document outward, not from the consumer brand inward.

The Phase 4 FM-04 test for this territory is a formality. A bank procurement portal simulation will confirm what the visual logic already argues: a brand that looks like a regulatory filing does not need to "adapt" to the institutional context. It already lives there.

Naming Compatibility

The Record's naming requires a word that sounds right in an official document header — the kind of word that precedes a filing number.

Brief
Strongest fit
An intelligence brief is the document that precedes the action — the distilled record of what the platform observed, sourced, and documented. In The Record territory, the brief IS the deliverable. "Brief by Lede" in Schibsted Grotesk All-Caps reads as a filing header. The name and the aesthetic are the same claim: this is the intelligence document.
Bearing
Strong fit
Navigation's term for documented orientation — "get your bearing" = "read the record." In a document, a bearing is a position finding, not just a direction. "Bearing by Lede" in the document header reads clearly and professionally.
Vigil
Adequate fit
Continuous monitoring — a vigil produces records. The connotation is about the watching, not the document, which creates a slight register tension in a territory built around the artifact. "Vigil by Lede" is plausible in the filing header but positions the brand as the watcher, not the record. Works; not the strongest pairing.
Notes for Phase 3 Evaluator
Dimension Observation
Risk The document aesthetic may test as "boring" with PM and CTO audiences, who are accustomed to product-led brand aesthetics. A compliance officer will love this territory; an analyst may find it cold. This is a tradeoff between institutional credibility (highest in this territory) and product excitement (lowest in this territory).
Distinctiveness No major institutional fintech brand uses a warm-paper document aesthetic as its brand register (as of August 2026). FactSet and Bloomberg are both dark or neutral. Morningstar uses color blocks. The Record territory occupies an unoccupied visual position — the risk is whether "unoccupied" means "distinctive" or "avoided for good reason."
Relationship to consumer Lede Moderate visual separation. The warm-paper ground shares palette DNA with the consumer brand's brand register (#F7F5EF / #EFECE2), but the architectural treatment is completely different. Newsreader as the body face is in the consumer token system (already licensed as a reading face). There is more continuity here than in Territory A — this may or may not be desirable depending on the Phase 4 architecture decision.
Marketing surfaces The document aesthetic on a conference-room screen, a Bloomberg ad unit, or a LinkedIn carousel is distinctive and unusual. The risk is whether it reads as "premium" or "stodgy." Phase 4 should test this territory on those specific surfaces before final selection.
