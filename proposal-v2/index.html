01 The letter

You are not an AI company that found finance. You are a judgment company that found machines — and that distinction is worth more than any logo we could draw you.

Here is what we think you actually sell. Alfa reads more than any analyst who ever lived — filings, transcripts, tape, the whole exhaust of the market — and hands a portfolio manager not answers but defensible positions: ranked, cited, priced with their own uncertainty. Your buyers are people whose careers are marked to market daily, and platforms whose retail users deserve better than a sentiment emoji. In both cases the product is the same thing: trusted judgment under uncertainty. Nobody buys that from a brand that sounds like an energy drink with a domain hack.

And yet — the uncomfortable, useful truth in your history is that you were founded on showing the work. Gradient-boosted trees were the 2017 choice of teams who refused black boxes; your legal name is a citation to explainability. The method aged. The reason for the method is now the most valuable positioning in your category, and it is sitting unclaimed in your certificate of incorporation.

So we didn’t write you a process deck. Process decks are how studios describe taste they can’t demonstrate. Instead we started the engagement three weeks early, at our own cost: the audit is in this document, the competitive field is mapped, and the three creative territories are not described — they are built, each section of this page rendered in its own system, with real copy and real values throughout. Where a comp appears, it is a comp we would defend in the room.

One more thing, said plainly because you asked for plainness in Section 6: we arrive with a bet, not a menu. Three territories follow, mutually exclusive by design. At the end we tell you which one we’d stake our name on, what it risks, and exactly what evidence would change our mind. Agencies that present three options without conviction are asking you to do their job.

Etta Vance
Founder & creative director, Fair Copy

02 Brand audit — started early, at our cost

Eight years of engineering discipline, wearing a 2018 costume.

We reconstructed the current brand from the outside in: the live site, the sales deck voice, and — most tellingly — the product’s actual design tokens, which your team polices with more rigor than most brands police their logos. The findings, politely but without anesthetic:

The name(s)Exhibit A

You don’t have a name. You have three, and none of them has the job.

The invoice says Gradient Boosted Investments. The market says Boosted.ai. Your users say Alfa — because that’s what’s open on their second monitor at 6:40 a.m. Three names, no architecture: the company brand carries the conferences, the product brand carries the affection, and the legal name carries the only honest claim (explainability) where no buyer will ever read it.

“Boosted” is a method name, and methods age — transformers ate the trees years ago. To an engineer it’s a citation; to a PM it reads as a caffeine brand; to a compliance officer it reads as a promise you can’t make (“boosted” returns?). And the “.ai” suffix, differentiating in 2019, is now the least differentiating thing a company can say — table stakes worn as identity. The suffix also costs you in the new discovery layer: answer engines resolve entities, and “Boosted” alone is un-ownable in search without a qualifier you don’t control.

03 The landscape

Everyone in your category is wearing a costume. There are two racks.

AI-research brands dress either as the machine (lab minimalism, model-talk, off-white and orbs) or as the market (dark suits, serif gravitas, terminal cosplay). And nearly all of them share one posture: the oracle — trust the output, don’t ask how. We mapped the field on those two axes. The empty quadrant is not subtle.

AlphaSenseThe incumbent’s incumbent

Owns “search everything” for the enterprise. Brand is procurement-safe navy — authority borrowed from the software category, not the market. Nobody loves it; everybody renews it.

HebbiaThe seminar room

Ivy-serious, off-white, matrix-shaped. Speaks fluent model, addresses the analyst as an operator of AI rather than an owner of judgment. Oracle posture with better tailoring.

RogoThe costume, committed

Dark, serif, cufflinked — Wall Street re-enacted. Effective theater, but theater: the brand asks to be trusted the old way while selling the new thing. Nothing shows its work.

BrightwaveThe optimist

Gradient warmth, agent language, demo-forward. Reads YC, not buy side. Will iterate fast; will not out-institution you unless you leave institution unclaimed.

Bloomberg / KenshoThe landlord

The only earned authority on the map — and it’s data authority, not judgment. The Terminal shows everything and concludes nothing. That gap is your inheritance if you claim it.

The ambient aestheticThe purple sea

Mesh gradients, orbs, “your AI copilot for…”. This is the water the category swims in. Any territory we ship must be checkably far from it — it is the one direction we refuse to walk.

The whitespace, in one sentence: nobody in this field has built the brand that says “don’t trust us — check us,” in the market’s own language. The product already behaves that way. The brand work is to stop hiding it.

04 Strategic platform

The platform in two words:

Auditable conviction.

Positioning statement

For professionals paid to be right about uncertainty — buy-side PMs and analysts, and the platforms serving self-directed investors — Boosted is the research intelligence that shows its work: machine-scale reading, human-checkable reasoning, conviction that arrives with receipts. Against black-box copilots on one side and retrieval terminals on the other, the promise is not “answers.” It is defensible positions.

Why this holds

It is the only claim in the category the product can prove on contact — every citation chip, every explanation pane, every token-level act of restraint is evidence. It serves both audiences without translation: a PM hears edge I can defend to my IC; a brokerage platform hears diligence my compliance team can approve. And it weaponizes your founding story instead of apologizing for it.

Three traits — each with its tension left in

Confident, never certain

The brand states positions plainly and prices its own doubt in the same breath. Probabilities are spoken, not buried in tooltips. Certainty is the tell of a machine that hasn’t been checked; we never fake it — that’s the difference between conviction and salesmanship.

Fast, never breathless

First to know, last to shout. Speed is table stakes; composure is the luxury good. No countdown urgency, no exclamation marks, no “🚨”. The brand moves like someone who was already awake when the news broke.

Industrial scale, desk-side manners

Reads six thousand filings a night; talks to you like the best analyst on the desk — cites sources, admits misses, answers the follow-up question before it’s asked. Machine capability with the etiquette of a colleague who’d like to keep the job.

Voice principles — with the current copy corrected in place

1 · Numbers before adjectives

If a sentence can carry a figure, it must. “Powerful” is what you say when you can’t say “6,000 filings, 40 minutes.”

2 · Cite or delete

Every claim behaves like the product: it shows a source or it doesn’t ship. This applies to marketing as ruthlessly as to model output.

3 · Plain verbs, no velvet

Banned on sight: harness, leverage, unlock, supercharge, empower, revolutionize. Allowed: read, rank, cite, check, miss, revise.

Before — boosted.ai, homepage hero

“Harness the power of explainable AI to supercharge your investment research.”

After

“Read 6,000 filings before your second coffee. Every claim cited.”

Before — product marketing, Alfa

After

Before — social proof strip

After

What follows is the platform worn three ways. The strategy above is constant; the territory decides which trait leads, who it wins first, and what the company must be brave about. Calibration

Art direction — if it isn’t a measurement, it doesn’t ship
  1. Data is the only imagery. No people, no offices, no metaphor photography. Every visual is a real chart, a real distribution, a real ranked table — with real values. If legal wouldn’t let us print the number, we don’t fake one; we find one we can print.
  2. Units, always. bps, σ, N, ±, ET. A number without a unit is decoration, and decoration is banned.
  3. Charts are instruments, not posters. Single series emphasized, full axes, visible baselines, confidence bands drawn honestly. Grid lines at 8% opacity. No glow, no area-fill gradients, no truncated axes — the brand never flatters a chart.
  4. Motion is the needle. One gesture: the scale draws left-to-right (550ms), the tick lands (200ms), numbers settle like tape. Once per event, never looping. Quiet surfaces hold still. prefers-reduced-motion renders final states.

Territory 03 · of 03

The Bureau

Thesis: break the category entirely — leave “AI for finance” and become the market’s weather service. Forecasts with their odds attached, published in daylight, by the first finance brand honest enough to be graded in public.

Who it wins

Retail brokerage platforms first — plain-spoken probabilistic honesty is exactly what their compliance and their users both want. Press and category authorship. The half of the market exhausted by oracles.

What it risks

A rename: cost, migration, and trademark roulette. Metaphor discipline — one whimsical misstep and the weather service becomes a weather app. Institutions may read daylight as retail.

Architecture

New master brand (funnel below, vetting flagged); Boosted retires to the legal entity; Alfa persists as the instrument the bureau runs. The biggest move on the table — priced as such.

The name — a funnel in progress, shown honestly

This territory requires a name that does what “Boosted” can’t. We do not pretend the funnel is finished — 24 candidates entered, three survive, all pending linguistic screens (EN/FR/JP/DE/PT) and class 36/42 knockouts. Two kills shown because the reasoning is the deliverable:
