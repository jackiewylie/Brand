(01) The idea

One line. Flat is honesty. The step is the event.

When material information hits a market, a price chart does one thing: it gaps — a step. That step is the most truthful shape in finance: the visible moment when someone knew something new. Lede's entire visual system is built from that shape. A continuous line runs through the brand — watching. Most of the time it is perfectly flat, because most days nothing material happens, and this brand refuses to pretend otherwise. When something matters, the line steps, the corner takes an orange node, and the moment gets a timestamp. The identity is the product's one job, drawn.

Everything derives from it: the logomark is one step. The wordmark carries its own line. Dividers are quiet lines that step once. Charts, underlines, progress — the same stroke. Motion is the line behaving. The quiet day — no steps — is rendered proudly, not apologetically: a flat line with the STET stamp (§04).

(02) Logo — the Step

A mark you can build with a ruler and read as a chart.

Construction. One stroke, two right angles, on a 10-unit grid: the line enters flat, steps at the 5:12 division, exits higher. The corner carries the node — the moment itself, always in Flare. Rotate the step's corner and it is the letter L; the mark contains the monogram without asking for one. It survives at 16 pixels, embroiders on a cap, and animates by doing the only thing it knows: stepping.

Meaning check. Vercel's triangle is deployment as delta. Anthropic's mark is a burst of thought. The Step is new information arriving — no other investing brand can claim it without also claiming our job.

(03) Color — the black-box story

Flight recorders aren't black. They're orange, so they're never missed.

Aviation reserves international orange for the instruments that must be found first — flight recorders, flare guns, the Golden Gate's steel. It is engineering's color for attention that matters, and no investing brand owns it. Yellow is retired from this identity entirely: that field belongs to Ramp, and borrowed fields grow someone else's crop.

Flare at two percent is the entire discipline: on any surface, orange appears exactly where the event is — the node, the new number, the one action — and nowhere else. A surface with three orange things has zero ledes. One accent color total: v1's byline blue is retired; a brand about one thing that matters cannot have two ways of pointing.

(04) Typography — a voice, a worker, a clerk

High contrast when it speaks. No contrast when it counts.

Display — “Lede Serif”
(Instrument Serif interim)
Never the last to know. Never the first to shout.

Hairline contrast, sharp beaks, real italics — a voice for headlines, the wordmark, and the Morning Lede masthead. One weight, on purpose: emphasis comes from the italic and the line, never from bolding. Commission the exclusive cut in year one; the interim face even carries the right name.

Working — Schibsted Grotesk

Product UI, captions, controls, running interface text. Drawn for a news publisher, warm without softness. 400 / 500 / 700, mapped through the token cascade so product weight discipline survives intact.

Clerk — IBM Plex Mono

06:31 · NVDA · 8-K item 1.02 · $5.2T · tabular-nums always. Timestamps, tickers, figures, folios, the ration labels on this very page.

Long-form reading (briefs, research) sets in Newsreader — the text serif v1 promoted above its station now does the job it was drawn for.

(→) The STET stamp — the quiet day, certified

Stet — the proofreader's verdict: let it stand, nothing changes. It is the doctrine of quiet made physical. The stamp closes every quiet-day Morning Lede, prints on the back of the business card, and belongs on a tote before it belongs in a pitch deck. Engagement apps mint streak badges for opening the app; Lede stamps STET for the days you didn't need to. That inversion — celebrating the product's silence — is the most ownable brand behavior in the category.

One behavior, three rules: the line draws left-to-right in 550ms and the node pops at the corner — once, never looping in product. Numbers tick like tape. Quiet surfaces do not move. (The ambient hero line on brand surfaces is the sanctioned exception: environment, not interface.) Reduced-motion renders every mark in its final state.

(→) The short law, amended

  • Always
  • One step per surface; the node sits only on the corner, only in Flare
  • Flare at graphic sizes; Flare Text for words — contrast is non-negotiable
  • Display serif in one weight; emphasis by italic and line, never bolding
  • The stamp only on true quiet days — it is a certification, not a sticker
  • Ambient line on brand surfaces; product surfaces move only at events
  • Never
  • Yellow. Anywhere. That field is Ramp's
  • Two accents on one surface — one way of pointing, always
  • The step as decoration — it appears only where something actually happened
  • Market green/red as brand colors; they belong to data
  • Confetti, streaks, badges, mascots, gradients — unchanged from v1, still law
