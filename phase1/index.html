02

Naming Architecture Decision

The resolution of P0-A2 (naming architecture unresolved) and P0-B1/B3 (Alfa clock and Alfa Bank risk). This section makes seven discrete naming decisions, each with a documented rationale.

The problem at Phase 0. Seven names for one product: Alfa, Alpha, Alpha 3, Alpha Prime, AlphaHub, Ask Alpha, Intelligence. Five tier labels — Pro, Prime, Platform, Powered, Retail — nowhere defined. Two overlapping clocks: Alfa retirement and endorsement expiry. A potential institutional client who has heard "Alfa" at a conference, received a deck about "Alpha Prime," and seen a LinkedIn ad for "Boosted.ai" cannot confirm they're buying the same product. This section resolves that.

D1 — Architecture Model: Endorsed House

Decision D1 · Confirmed

The professional brand is an endorsed house: one company (Lede), two tracked brands (Lede consumer / [Professional Brand] by Lede)

The company is Lede (formerly Boosted.ai). The company endorses two distinct product brands: the consumer product (Lede, standalone) and the professional product ([Professional Brand] by Lede, currently Alfa by Lede). The two product brands are not siblings — they serve different buyers, different contexts, and different sales motions. They share the company endorsement and the token architecture. They do not share a visual register, a naming system, or a co-branding framework.

Rejected alternatives: (a) Unified master brand — "Lede Pro / Lede Prime / Lede Platform" — rejected because "Lede" is a journalism/editorial word that earns trust in consumer contexts; in fund board language, a product called "Lede" is unexplained. (b) Federated peers — Lede and Alfa as independent sibling brands — rejected because it fragments company equity and requires two brand systems to be maintained independently. (c) Permanent Alfa — rejected due to P0-B3 (Alfa Bank sanctions) and P0-B1 (naming clock).

LEDE (company · formerly Boosted.ai · Gradient Boosted Investments, Inc.) │ ├── LEDE (consumer product · standalone · $25/month · retail) │ └── [PROFESSIONAL BRAND] by Lede (named in Phase 2 · currently: Alfa) │ ├── PRO — individual analyst · self-serve or sales-assisted ├── PRIME — team license · multi-user · shared workspaces ├── PLATFORM — enterprise deployment · SSO · SOC 2 · SLA ├── POWERED — partner integration · co-branding system · attribution options └── POWERED+ — full white-label · no attribution · negotiated

Decision D3 · Conditional

Alfa continues in-channel through Q3 2027, conditional on legal opinion rating the Alfa Bank sanctions conflict manageable

If legal counsel rates the Alfa Bank / class 36/42 risk as low or manageable: Alfa continues with the vendor due diligence disambiguation protocol active. D5 — Phase 2 Naming Brief for Post-Alfa Professional Brand

Decision D5 · Brief delivered to Phase 2

The post-Alfa professional product name is selected in Phase 2, governed by seven criteria

Criterion Description Failure examples
1 · Entity clean Does not share a name with any entity on the OFAC SDN list, EU Consolidated Sanctions List, or UK OFSI list. Does not return a sanctioned financial institution in standard entity-check databases. Alfa (Alfa Bank), Horizon (sanctioned entities), Sigma (overloaded in fintech)
2 · Fund-manager legible Can appear in a board meeting, a prime broker agreement, or a fund auditor's vendor list without explanation. Not a consumer-brand word. Not an editorial metaphor. Lede, Early, Tenor (consumer/editorial), Tape (too niche)
3 · Works with "by Lede" The compound "[Name] by Lede" reads cleanly in both spoken and written form. "[Name] Platform" and "Powered by [Name]" must both work. "Ask [Name]" should work for the agent interface. Names that are verbs (makes "Ask" awkward), names that end in vowels (elide with "by")
4 · Finance-adjacent but not finance-literal Evokes precision, coverage, signal, or timing without being a common finance term that is already in use as an asset class, metric, or product category. Alpha (finance concept), Beta (finance concept), Signal (overused), Yield (overused)
5 · Speakable, 1–2 syllables preferred Pronounced without ambiguity in an English-speaking financial context. No silent letters. No homophone tax equivalent to "Lede / Lead." Maximum 2 syllables for the master name; tier descriptors can be longer. Bellwether (3 syllables, misspelling tax), Standfirst (obscure outside UK newsroom)
6 · Trademark class 36/42 viable Can be registered as a trademark in class 36 (financial services) and class 42 (software/SaaS) in US, UK, and EU without a high-conflict opposition risk from an existing registration. Must be tested — do not assume
7 · Product lexicon available "A [Name]" can be a noun for a thing the product produces or detects. "Your [Name]s" must be a natural plural in a professional research context. Names that work only as company names, not as product nouns

Seed territories for Phase 2 exploration (not commitments): names in the measurement / instrument / record / coverage domains — words that a fund manager's auditor would associate with precision rather than personality. Specifically: naming territories around the concepts of record (what is documented), watch (what is monitored), scope (what is covered), and mark (what is flagged). These territories avoid both finance-literal and consumer-editorial pitfalls.

Positioning Strategy

The professional brand's strategic position in the competitive landscape. Confirmed for Phase 2 creative development. Not a tagline brief — a decision about what the brand stands for and against.

D6 — Positioning Statement

Decision D6 · Confirmed

The professional brand owns the "intelligence layer" position: continuous, verifiable, coverage-complete

For professional investment teams
who need continuous, verifiable research coverage across their entire portfolio universe
without gaps in scope, speed, or source transparency,
[Professional Brand] by Lede is the AI investment intelligence platform
that monitors continuously, reasons traceably, and cites every source—
so nothing material ever goes unseen.

Three Strategic Claims (Priority Order)

Claim 01 · Primary · "Coverage without gaps"

The platform monitors every name in scope, every source in scope, continuously — not on request

This is the most precise articulation of the institutional value proposition available in any prior material. It is analyst-legible, compliance-credible, and CIO-relevant. It distinguishes Alfa from point-solution tools (which answer questions you ask) and from terminal data (which holds information you must retrieve). Carry this claim verbatim into all professional brand materials or improve it with specific evidence — do not replace it with vaguer language.

The proof: 10,000+ agents in production monitoring named securities and sectors. Pre-market briefings generated without user request. Material change detection across earnings transcripts, 8-K filings, guidance language, analyst notes, and market data. (All figures require human verification before public use.)

Claim 02 · Trust anchor · "Verifiable by design"

Every output cites its source. Every reasoning step is logged. Compliance does not need to trust the AI — they can audit it.

The product's deepest differentiator is auditable AI reasoning — not a brand promise but a technical architecture decision that becomes a trust claim when surfaced to institutional buyers. Source attribution at the output level transforms an AI tool from a black box into a documented instrument. This is the brand's answer to every enterprise procurement committee question about AI reliability.

The proof: Source citation in every output (link to primary document or data source). Audit trail architecture for Platform tier. Distinguishes "material fact from primary source" from "AI-generated synthesis." Output provenance chain available for compliance review.

Claim 03 · Brand register · "The instrument that never misses"

The flight-recorder story, adapted for institutional contexts: not an editorial metaphor, a precision instrument metaphor

This claim connects the consumer Lede identity (the Flare orange story: "the instrument that must never be missed") to the professional context without importing the consumer editorial aesthetic. The flight recorder is an institutional instrument: precise, mandatory, regulatory, and trusted by exactly the kind of compliance-minded buyer Alfa is targeting. The metaphor survives institutional scrutiny where the proofreader's mark metaphor does not.

Use of this claim: Brand register only — does not appear in sales materials or product UI. It is the emotional anchor for the visual language in Phase 2 creative territories, not a tagline for enterprise procurement documents.

The Competitor Swap Test — Applied to This Positioning

Confirmation: This positioning fails the competitor swap test — which is required. "Coverage without gaps" cannot be claimed by AlphaSense (reactive search), Hebbia (document answers), FactSet (library, not monitor), or Bloomberg (terminal, not intelligence system). "Verifiable by design" with output-level source citation and audit trail is a specific technical claim that most competitors either cannot make or have not centered. The "intelligence layer" position — continuous, between the terminal and the analyst's judgment — is an unoccupied competitive territory as of August 2026. A Phase 2 creative team must build a visual language that makes this position untransferable.
04

Audience-Specific Messaging Hierarchy

Six professional audiences, each with a primary claim, a fear, a proof point, and a tone protocol. These are inputs to Phase 2 copywriting, not taglines. Do not use this section to write marketing copy — use it to write the right marketing copy.

A critical gap from Phase 0. The prior engagement produced no audience-specific professional messaging. The brand has one institutional retention message ("Same engine, same team, sharper name. Nothing about your deployment changes but the letterhead.") and one professional hero headline ("The AI engine behind modern investing.") Neither is differentiated by professional role, buying stage, or use case. This section fills that gap.
Audience 01 · Primary professional buyer
Head of Research / Portfolio Manager
"Alfa surfaces what's portfolio-material before you know to ask."
What they fear

Missing a material development in a name they cover. Being the last to know about an earnings pre-announcement, a regulatory filing, a guidance change. Having a blank in coverage that a competitor exploits at the investment committee. Not because they weren't working — but because the signal was in the 47th document of the morning.

What they value

Speed to synthesis. Reliability of signal over volume of output. Credibility with the investment committee — the ability to say "we saw it, we verified it, here's the source."

Proof points
  • Pre-market briefings generated without user request — every morning, your watchlist, sources cited
  • Material change detection before earnings calls — thesis drift, guidance language shifts, unusual volume
  • Source cited in every output — no black-box synthesis
Tone protocol

Peer-to-peer. No "empower" or "transform." Never imply they weren't doing their job before. Never suggest the AI decides — it surfaces; the PM decides. "Before the open" not "accelerate your workflow." Outcome language: "you saw it first" not "we found it for you."

Forbidden phrases

game-changing, revolutionary, AI-powered, seamless, next-generation, insights platform, decision intelligence

Audience 02 · Senior economic buyer
Chief Investment Officer
"Coverage without gaps. Your research infrastructure scales with the mandate — not with headcount."
What they fear

Research infrastructure that doesn't scale with the portfolio mandate. A team equipped for 80 names managing 200. A single researcher as a coverage risk — the fund's research capability is one departure away from a gap. LP pressure on operational efficiency.

What they value

Operational scale. Defensibility to the investment committee and LPs. Coverage documentation. A research infrastructure story that sounds like operational discipline, not AI experimentation.

Proof points
  • 10,000+ agents in production (requires human verification)
  • $5T+ AUM on platform (requires human verification of current figure and definition)
  • 8 years of institutional deployment — not a pilot, a production system
Tone protocol

Business-case framing. Operational. Defensible to a board. Never "AI-first" — "research infrastructure." Never imply replacing the research team — always "scales the team's coverage." AUM and agent counts establish scale; do not over-rely on them without verification.

Forbidden phrases

augment your team, AI copilot, intelligent assistant, supercharge, unlock insights

Audience 03 · Technical buyer
Chief Technology Officer / VP Technology
"Every output carries its full provenance chain. Deploy, audit, verify."
What they fear

AI vendor lock-in with no exit path. AI outputs that fail audit because they cannot be traced to a source. Deployment instability in production. Data leakage. Security certification that exists in a PDF but not in a live system. A vendor who is small enough to fail before the contract expires.

What they value

Auditability. Interoperability (can this sit next to Aladdin, Bloomberg, FactSet?). SOC 2 certification. Deployment flexibility. Clear API contracts with versioned stability. Named support contacts. A vendor due diligence package that is already written.

Proof points
  • Source citation at output level — every synthesis traces to a primary document or data source
  • Audit trail architecture in Platform tier — compliance review without calling support
  • SOC 2 compliance documentation (requires human verification — current status and scope)
  • Deployment options: cloud, on-prem, or hybrid (requires human verification)
  • API with versioned stability commitment (requires human verification of terms)
Tone protocol

Technical. Precise. Stripe-like documentation aesthetic — meticulous, not designed-to-impress. Show, don't assert. Every claim needs a reference: "SOC 2 Type II, scope: [list]" not "enterprise-grade security." The CTO is looking for the thing that will fail at 2 AM — address that thing.

Forbidden phrases

AI-powered (without specifics), enterprise-grade (without documentation), cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, military-grade encryption (unless literally true)

Audience 04 · Regulatory buyer
Chief Compliance Officer / General Counsel
"Alfa produces research inputs, not investment decisions. Every source is documented. Every output is reproducible."
What they fear

AI-generated research treated as investment advice or a recommendation in a regulatory audit. Failure to document how a trading decision was reached when an AI tool was in the workflow. MNPI / Reg FD obligations triggered by an AI that ingests non-public information without a clear policy. A vendor who is not aware of their compliance exposure.

What they value

Clear scope definition: what the product produces and what it is not. Output classification (fact vs. synthesis vs. alert). Audit trail for compliance review. Disclosed methodology. Separation from trading decisions documented in writing. A vendor who can answer the regulator's question before the CCO finishes asking it.

Proof points
  • Output classification taxonomy: material fact (cited primary source) / AI-generated synthesis (labeled) / monitored alert (triggered by defined conditions)
  • Complete source attribution — every output links to the originating document or data source
  • Audit log accessible for compliance review without requiring a support ticket
  • MNPI handling policy (requires human verification — current policy must be confirmed and documented)
  • The product does not make investment decisions. It surfaces research inputs. The compliance framing is "research tool," not "advisory system."
Tone protocol

Precise, legal-grade language. No marketing adjectives unless they are legal terms. Never "insight" (legally ambiguous in investment contexts). Never "recommendation." Never "advice." The CCO is reading to find the thing that will create a compliance problem — acknowledge that thing and explain how it is handled before they ask.

Forbidden phrases

investment advice, recommendations, insights (as a product noun), predictive (without a specific definition), real-time (unless technically true and defined)

Audience 05 · Procurement buyer
Vendor Management / Procurement
"Alfa is enterprise-ready. The documentation is already written. Your review starts on page one."
What they fear

A vendor who cannot pass security review without three months of follow-up questions. Unclear contractual scope that creates disputes at renewal. Hidden data usage that violates their data policy. Vendor exit risk — what happens to their data if the vendor is acquired or fails?

What they value

A complete vendor due diligence package that anticipates their checklist. Standard DPA template that their legal team can sign without significant redlines. SOC 2. Data residency clarity. SLA terms. Named enterprise support contact. A vendor who treats procurement as a professional function, not an obstacle.

Proof points
  • Vendor due diligence package: pre-written, covering security, data handling, compliance, deployment, SLA
  • Standard DPA template (requires human verification — current document must be confirmed available)
  • Entity disambiguation notice (D4 — required for Alfa; resolves the Alfa Bank entity check)
  • Named enterprise support contact and escalation path
  • Data deletion and exit procedure documented
Tone protocol

Document-forward. Professional. Anticipate the checklist before it is issued. The procurement audience is not buying the product — they are de-risking it. Every claim must be backed by a document. "SOC 2 Type II" not "enterprise security." The brand experience for procurement is the quality of the vendor package, not the landing page.

Forbidden phrases

platform (without specification), solution, best-in-class, industry-leading, trusted by leading firms (unless named and approved)

Audience 06 · Integration buyer · Platform / Powered tier
Technology Partner / Platform Vendor / Bank Portal Team
"Six co-presence models. Attribution from invisible to co-branded equal weight — your call."
What they fear

Co-branding that diminishes their own brand identity in their own product. Technical integration risk with a vendor whose API stability is uncertain. Attribution requirements that they didn't agree to being baked into the product's design. A "Powered by" designation that reads as a sub-brand rather than a technology attribution.

What they value

Design system compatibility — can this engine be embedded without visible seams? API stability with versioned contracts. Attribution flexibility — the ability to choose how (and whether) the engine provider's name appears. A named partnership team who will still be there at the next contract renewal.

Proof points
  • Six explicitly designed co-presence scenarios — detailed in Phase 7 brand system (not yet built; this is the Phase 7 deliverable)
  • Design system delivered as a token set that maps to the partner's existing design system — not as static mockups
  • API with semantic versioning and deprecation notice period (requires human verification of terms)
  • Named partnership team (requires human verification of current team structure)
Tone protocol

Partnership-framing. Empowering to the partner brand. Specific about what "Powered by" means (it is a designed system with options) and what it does not mean (it is not mandatory branding — Powered+ has no attribution). The engine is a tool the partner uses; the partner's success is the measure of the relationship.

Forbidden phrases

partner ecosystem, co-innovation, synergy, white-glove (unless that is the literal service tier)

Brand Voice & Verbal Standards

Extension of the institutional voice standard from the prior engagement. The existing standard is confirmed and extended with professional-specific rules. This section does not replace the delivery book's voice work — it adds the professional layer that was not in scope.

Confirmed from Prior Engagement

The institutional voice standard survives. Specific over vague. Outcome-led. The real banned-words list (game-changing, revolutionary, seamless, transform). "Coverage without gaps" as a precise claim. These are confirmed for professional use and extended below.

Professional Voice Principles

Principle What it means in practice Example: wrong → right
Claim with evidence or don't claim Every factual assertion about the product's capabilities must be backed by a specific metric, a documented output, or an auditable example. "Comprehensive coverage" is forbidden. "Coverage of 10,000+ named securities across 47 markets" is permitted — if verified. "Industry-leading accuracy" → "Source cited in every output. Verify it yourself."
The product surfaces; the analyst decides Never claim the product makes decisions, recommends actions, or predicts outcomes. The product is an instrument that surfaces material information. The analyst applies judgment. This is not a legal hedge — it is an accurate description of the product and a respect for the buyer's expertise. "Alfa recommends reducing your position" → "Alfa flagged a material guidance change in the 10-Q filed this morning."
Specificity is credibility In institutional contexts, vagueness reads as either ignorance or evasion. Name the filing type. Name the detection method. Name the source. "Something changed" is not a sentence the brand uses. "The company revised full-year guidance downward by 12% in the 8-K filed at 8:47 AM" is. "We detected a change in company outlook" → "Guidance revised -12% in 8-K filed 8:47 AM ET. Source: SEC EDGAR."
Quiet confidence, not performance The brand does not shout. It does not use exclamation marks in professional materials. It does not use "!" in enterprise documentation. The confidence is in the specificity of the claim, not the volume of the assertion. "Alfa is the most powerful AI research platform available!" → "Coverage without gaps. Nothing material escapes unread."
The audience is not a target — they are a peer Buy-side analysts, heads of research, and CIOs are among the most analytically sophisticated professionals in any industry. They will read through vague claims immediately. The brand writes to them as peers — not as subjects of persuasion. "Even experienced investors miss critical signals" → [deleted; condescending]

Permitted / Forbidden by Context

Professional Brand Color Constraint

A strategic constraint on the use of Flare orange in professional contexts. Not a palette decision — palette is Phase 4. This section defines what the professional color system must not do, regardless of what creative direction Phase 2 selects.

Decision D7 · Confirmed

Flare orange is permitted in professional contexts at ≤2% of surface, appearance-earned — never as a decorative accent

The v2.0.0 token spec specifies: "ONE signal color (Flare, international orange) — the node/step/action only, rationed at ~2% of any surface." This constraint is confirmed for professional contexts with an additional requirement: in professional surfaces, the orange may only appear where something actually happened. It marks an agent action, a material event detection, a new coverage signal. It does not appear as a border color, a button hover, a card background, or a section header.

The differentiation from Bloomberg orange is not achieved by hue adjustment — the values are too close for a visual distinction to carry the argument. It is achieved by context of use: Bloomberg orange is interface chrome (the Terminal's navigation, controls, and data chrome). Flare orange is a semantic event signal (something material changed). This distinction must be made explicit in every professional brand context where the orange appears. Phase 2 creative territories must either: (a) confirm the orange with this constraint applied and a prepared Bloomberg differentiation answer, or (b) propose an alternative professional accent that preserves the Step logomark's semantic logic without the Bloomberg proximity risk.

Constraints That Apply Regardless of Phase 2 Color Decision

Constraint Rationale
No drop shadows on professional surfaces Hard rule from v2.0.0 token spec. Shadow-free discipline is the design system's most distinctive constraint. "Enterprise seriousness" is not achieved by adding visual depth — it is achieved by surface-stepped elevation and information density.
No card borders on professional surfaces Same hard rule. Cards are separated by surface elevation (stepped background values), not by drawn borders. A professional brand that adds card borders to signal "enterprise" has misunderstood the system's logic.
WCAG 2.2 AA required on all professional surfaces Both the dark product register and any professional light register must pass WCAG 2.2 AA contrast testing before Phase 4 is complete. In a dense research UI, accent color that reads as "up" or "down" destroys the semantic clarity of the interface.
IBM Plex Mono for all numeric values, tickers, and timestamps Non-negotiable in all professional surfaces. tabular-nums required wherever digits line up in columns.
