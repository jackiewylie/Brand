# THREE CREATIVE TERRITORIES: BOOSTED.AI REBRAND

---

## TERRITORY 1: INK & CONVICTION

**Light Editorial — "A serious financial publication, rebuilt as software."**

---

**TERRITORY NAME:** Ink & Conviction

**TAGLINE:** "The analysis that moves institutional portfolios. Now open."

---

**VISUAL DIRECTION**

Background: Pure white (#FFFFFF) with a faint warm tint (#FAFAF9) as the secondary surface. The whiteness is a deliberate choice, not a default — it says we have nothing to hide and no atmosphere to manufacture.

Primary text color: Near-black (#0D0C0C) — not pure digital black, one hair warmer to reference print.

ONE accent color: Cobalt Ink (#1B3BCC). Not navy (too corporate America), not electric blue (too enterprise SaaS), not the current gold. This specific cobalt reads as fountain pen ink, editorial correction marks, the color of annotation on a well-read research note. It carries the authority of a byline without the clichés of "intelligence" or "technology."

Typography approach: Serif display paired with a precise grotesque for interface. Lyon Text or Freight Display for headlines — these have intellectual weight without stuffiness. ABC Diatype or Neue Haas Grotesk for data and navigation. The contrast is the whole message: this is a reading experience that happens to be software, not software that happens to contain text.

Signature visual gesture: Thin hairline column rules dividing content into editorial sections, like a newspaper broadsheet. Data appears as annotated prose — numbers embedded in sentence context — not as standalone charts. When the cobalt appears, it marks a specific piece of intelligence: a figure that changed, a recommendation, a signal. The rest of the page is black and white. One thing in cobalt per view.

Homepage hero: Full white field. Left-aligned serif headline, large but not monumental: "HSBC analysts stopped using ChatGPT for earnings prep. Here's what they use." Below it: three narrow columns of dense, real-looking financial prose divided by hairline rules. The cobalt appears exactly once, on a single number. No photography. No illustration. No gradients. The content is the design.

---

**EMOTIONAL CHARACTER**

Precise. Unimpressed. Credible. Mature. Authoritative.

This brand doesn't perform intelligence — it demonstrates it. The restraint of white says more than a dark backdrop ever could. The feel is a company that has been publishing serious financial analysis for decades and recently became software. It doesn't explain what Alfa is; it simply presents what Alfa says, and lets the quality speak. There is no "onboarding" energy, no gamification signal, no dopamine design. This reads as an institution that admitted individuals.

---

**STRATEGIC FIT**

For institutional buyers: White-and-editorial is the visual language of Bloomberg Opinion, the Financial Times, the Wall Street Journal. There is no "startup energy" that triggers compliance-team skepticism. The CIO encounter with this brand is: "finally, something that looks like the kind of research we actually read."

For the DRI: The publication feel creates aspiration without intimidation. The DRI already follows serious financial newsletters. This looks like that world — the premium research their fund manager's analyst reads — made into a product they can touch. The white is also mobile-native in a way dark screens are not. Reading in sunlight, in the morning, during commute: this is designed for that.

---

**COMPETITIVE DIFFERENTIATION**

vs. Bloomberg/FactSet: More readable, dramatically more human. A Bloomberg terminal has 400 things visible simultaneously. This direction has five.

vs. Robinhood/Wealthsimple: Not competing. Robinhood is green, rounded, dopamine-reward-patterned. This is ink and columns and editorial density. They are different products by appearance alone.

vs. Public.com: Public's palette is warmer neutral-gray, approachable. This is sharper, more assertive. Public feels like a well-designed investing forum. This feels like a private analyst sending you a brief.

vs. Kalshi (reference brand): Kalshi's white is bold and confident but generic-editorial. This direction adds the cobalt-ink layer and the serif typographic system, making it more recognizably "finance intelligence" rather than "prediction market."

---

**RISK/WEAKNESS**

The honest case against this direction: it can register as too quiet in competitive digital environments. In a mobile feed, white-editorial blends with Axios, Seeking Alpha, Morning Brew. The DRI already pays for four financial newsletters; without extraordinarily strong copywriting and a typographically distinctive treatment, this becomes "another premium news reader" rather than an AI research platform. The serif-plus-grotesque system also demands real typographic craft to maintain across mobile UIs — done poorly, it just looks like a blog. The brand name and copy have to do heavy lifting here. If the company keeps a name that sounds startup-adjacent, this direction has nowhere to rest its credibility.

## TERRITORY 2: THE BOUND EDITION

**Dark Refined — "The typographic intelligence that institutions keep."**

---

**TERRITORY NAME:** The Bound Edition

**TAGLINE:** "Research worth keeping."

---

**VISUAL DIRECTION**

Background: Deep warm charcoal (#191510) — this specific hex is the territory. Critically, it has a slight brown undertone. It is not midnight blue (#0D1421), not pure cold black (#080808 — the current brand). It is the color of a leather-bound research compendium left open in a dim reading room. Warm, not cold. That distinction is the entire emotional argument against the current brand.

Secondary surface: (#221D18) — for panels, cards, and subtle layering. Just warm enough to feel like a second page.

Primary text color: Warm cream (#F0EAD8) — not white. The warmth reduces eye strain and signals "designed for reading" rather than "designed for selling." The effect is a physical book rendered in software.

ONE accent color: Claret (#8C1F2E). Not red — claret. The distinction is load-bearing. Red means urgency, alarm, danger. Claret means The Economist's masthead, the ribbon on a debenture certificate, the spine of an important first edition. It signals authority that has never needed to raise its voice. This color appears only on: brand marks, active interactive states, and one significant number or signal per view. Nothing decorative.

Typography approach: This direction lives or dies on one typographic choice. Canela or Editorial New — both from Commercial Type — for display headlines. These faces have the warmth of old serif typefaces and the precision of modern ones. No swashes, no antique filigree, no period-costume associations. Paired with a wider, calm grotesque (Söhne, Aktiv Grotesk, or similar) for body copy and data. The serif carries the personality. The grotesque is the instrument. The two voices together say: considered depth with practical precision.

Signature visual gesture: The drop cap. Opening paragraphs of Alfa-generated analysis, of onboarding, of key reports — each begins with a large serif initial set in claret. This is a deeply unusual move in software. It signals that this content received the design attention usually reserved for literature. Supplementary gesture: thick hairline rules above section headers, used like newspaper column rules on a page worth reading slowly.

Homepage hero: Warm dark field. Serif headline in cream, tight leading, modest size: "The research that moved institutional portfolios. Open now." Below: a single simulated Alfa output — a typeset research note in the full typography system, showing what the product actually says. Not a UI screenshot. Not an abstract feature graphic. A document. The claret appears once, on the ticker or the confidence score. Nothing else above the fold.

---

**EMOTIONAL CHARACTER**

Considered. Serious. Warm. Unhurried. Quietly exclusive.

This brand communicates that it was built slowly, carefully, and entirely for people who take investing seriously. The visual register is a private family office's research library, a long-form financial memoir, an annual report that was actually designed by someone with taste. The DRI encounters it and thinks: "this was made for someone I want to become." The institutional buyer thinks: "finally, something that doesn't look like our Bloomberg terminal or a Series B pitch deck." The warmth is doing specific strategic work — it makes a dark brand feel earned rather than manufactured, literary rather than cinematic.

---

**STRATEGIC FIT**

For institutional buyers: The dark-refined register preserves the gravitas that enterprise procurement processes unconsciously require. A CIO shows this to a board member and it reads as considered, not scrappy. But it's completely different from the current brand's cold-fintech-dark, which says "we are a data terminal." This says "we are a research function."

For the DRI: The warmth is the emotional fulcrum. Cold dark is aspirational but distancing — you admire it from across the room. Warm dark with book typography says "you belong here." This is the difference between reading a Bloomberg terminal and reading a really well-designed book about the Bloomberg terminal. One is work; one is pleasure.

---

**COMPETITIVE DIFFERENTIATION**

vs. Bloomberg: Bloomberg dark means density and speed, the terminal aesthetic. This dark means depth and reading. They are not in the same visual category.

vs. Robinhood/Acorns: Not competing. Robinhood is gamified bright-dark; this is the difference between a casino sports book and a private library.

vs. Harvey (the reference brand): Harvey executes dark-refined beautifully for legal. The risk is this reads as "Harvey for finance" — a derivative creative move. The differentiator: Harvey is near-monochromatic dark with minimal typographic personality. This mixes warm brown tones and leads with a distinctive serif system Harvey does not have. The claret gives this something Harvey explicitly avoids: a color. Used right, the claret is the identity; Harvey's identity is the absence of one.

vs. the current Boosted brand: Completely different emotional world. Same tonal register (dark), entirely different character. Current: cold, geometric, Hanken Grotesk light-weight, gold accent. Territory 2: warm, typographic, serif-personality, claret accent. These are not incremental differences.

---

**RISK/WEAKNESS**

The honest case against this direction: "warm dark editorial" is increasingly a design trend in the premium fintech-adjacent space — Mercury, certain Brex executions, various wealth management rebrand exercises of the last 18 months. This direction risks being the "correct" answer that a brand consultant would give, which means it could feel over-designed and slightly precious rather than genuinely original. There is also a real question about the DRI's encounter with this brand via Instagram or TikTok — the literary/book register was built for readers, and 32-year-old individual investors who found Public.com through a podcast ad may not have the reference points to decode what this is offering. Strong serif choices also render poorly at small sizes, which stresses the mobile-first consumer product. Finally: this direction requires extraordinary consistency to maintain. One product manager who swaps the serif for Helvetica in a feature announcement collapses the whole identity.

## TERRITORY 3: THE OFFICIAL RECORD

**Unexpected — "Break the playbook. File the proof."**

---

**TERRITORY NAME:** The Official Record

**TAGLINE:** "The financial intelligence institutions kept off the market. Filed."

---

**VISUAL DIRECTION**

Background: Warm parchment (#E8E0CE) — this is the unexpected move. Not white (Territory 1). Not dark (Territory 2). The color of premium bond paper, of a Form S-1 cover, of a legal document that mattered. In a world of immaculate screens and dark mode, this surface carries material texture through reference alone. It says: this has been written down. It exists. It is on record.

Primary text color: Very deep warm ink (#0C0906) — near-black but brown-undertoned, printed rather than illuminated.

ONE accent color: Electric Violet (#4718DC). This is the deliberate surprise. Not navy (every bank). Not green (every trading platform). Not red (every alert system). Not gold (the current brand). Not cobalt (Territory 1). Not claret (Territory 2). Electric violet has zero existing major finance brand associations. It reads as: AI without being artificial, precision without coldness, signal inside a document. Used at full saturation, always in bold, always typographically — never decoratively. It appears specifically on model-generated signals, scored recommendations, and Alfa outputs. The violet IS the AI layer, visually segregated from everything else. When you see it, you know it came from the model.

Typography approach: One typeface. One weight variation. This is the binding constraint and the entire graphic argument. The direction uses a single condensed grotesque — Barlow Condensed or Aktiv Grotesk Condensed — at three scale registers: massive (display, 96-120pt equivalent), normal (body, 16pt), and very small (data, 10pt). No serifs. No italics. No other typographic variation. All visual hierarchy comes from scale contrast alone. This is extreme and unusual. A homepage using this system looks like architectural drawings and government filings and Swiss International Style posters simultaneously. None of those references are finance. That is the point.

Signature visual gesture: The stamp. A bold, clean rectangular block element — like a regulatory stamp, a form field, a compliance mark — used as the container for key data and Alfa outputs. When the model generates a high-conviction signal, a price target, or an earnings revision, it appears inside a violet-bordered rectangular stamp. This references financial documents (prospectuses, SEC filings, fund factsheets) without being literal about it. The stamp says: this is not a chatbot response. This has been assessed, classified, and filed. The brand carries a stamped quality — authoritative, definitive, on record.

Homepage hero: Warm parchment field. Top of page: the company name set in very large condensed black type, compressed horizontally, almost touching the left and right edges of the container. Maximum presence, no decoration. Below: one line at normal scale, no punctuation other than a period: "The financial intelligence that institutions pay seven figures for. Open now." Below that: three stamped rectangular blocks, each containing a single real claim. "40 hours to 20 minutes." "300+ institutional clients. $5T AUM." "8/10 prefer Alfa over ChatGPT." Each stamp is outlined in electric violet. Nothing else above the fold. The page breathes because everything not on it was removed.

---

**EMOTIONAL CHARACTER**

Declarative. Architectural. Confident. Documentarian. Slightly severe.

This brand does not explain itself. It states. It files. It moves on. The parchment-and-violet system says: we have read every filing, every prospectus, every research note produced in the last decade, and we built something that processes it better than anything else. The severity is not coldness — it's the confidence of something that has been verified. The DRI encounters this and feels like they have just been handed access to something that was previously restricted. The institutional buyer sees the document visual language, the stamp system, the filing cadence, and recognizes the visual world they work in — but rendered as something they have never seen before.

---

**STRATEGIC FIT**

For institutional buyers: Financial institutions live in documents. Compliance filings, research reports, fund prospectuses, regulatory submissions — the visual language of the official record is their native environment. Building a brand in that language creates unconscious familiarity while being completely unlike anything else in their vendor landscape. It does not say "institutional"; it simply exists in an institutional register without announcing it.

For the DRI: The parchment-and-violet combination is genuinely beautiful and unlike anything in the consumer finance category. The DRI has never seen a finance app designed like this. It reads as premium (the paper quality reference), modern (the violet AI layer), and confident (the stamped architectural system). It photographs very well on social — the compressed type at large scale produces images that look distinct in a feed full of gradient-heavy fintech creative.

---

**COMPETITIVE DIFFERENTIATION**

vs. Bloomberg/FactSet: They operate in terminal-dark and enterprise-blue. Parchment is so far outside their visual vocabulary it cannot be seen as a variant of what they do — it is a different category of object.

vs. Robinhood/Acorns/Betterment: Those brands reduce friction through softness — rounded corners, friendly colors, encouraging copy. This brand refuses softness entirely. That refusal is the competitive signal. This is for people who want to be serious investors, not comfortable ones.

vs. Public.com: Public uses warm neutrals in a conventional fintech layout — approachable and competent. The Official Record is more extreme, more opinionated, more singular. Public feels designed by a thoughtful product team. This feels designed by a designer with a genuine point of view.

vs. Kalshi (the reference brand): Kalshi's white-and-bold is the closest existing reference. Kalshi substitutes parchment for pure white (warmer, more material, more "official") and electric violet where Kalshi uses a clean primary blue. The condensed type system is also more compressed and architectural than Kalshi's editorial approach. The stamp gesture adds a layer of specificity Kalshi does not have.

---

**RISK/WEAKNESS**

The honest case against this direction: parchment backgrounds carry a double-edged association with official documents — it can read as modern-confident-archival or it can read as "trying to seem credible by looking old." In 18 months, this distinction may be clearer. The electric violet is a genuinely large bet. Violet is unusual in finance precisely because no major brand has made it work at scale in this category. If the execution is strong, it owns the color. If the execution is inconsistent, it reads as startup-adjacent or technology-adjacent (Figma, Notion, and various AI tools have explored purple territory). The condensed single-typeface system is also extremely restrictive for a product that must display multi-level financial data — earnings tables, options chains, research reports with nested hierarchies — and will require significant system-design investment to make the constraint work rather than break. Finally, the conceptual throughline ("the official record") requires the brand team to maintain extreme discipline across all touchpoints, forever. One off-brand email template or conference booth banner that uses standard corporate design collapses the entire system's coherence.
