# BOOSTED.AI FULL REBRAND: NAMING STRATEGY

---

## SECTION 1 — CURRENT NAME AUDIT

### "Boosted.ai"

**Memorability and pronounceability:** Perfectly pronounceable. Memorability is medium at best — "Boosted" is a common word applied across fitness, automotive, supplement, and social media contexts, none of which are useful associations here.

**Category fit:** Poor. "Boosted" implies enhancement of something that already exists rather than original intelligence or advantage. It positions the product as a modifier rather than a primary source of value. The ".ai" suffix, once a sharp differentiator, has become the most generic signal in tech — it now reads "startup" rather than "serious infrastructure." For a company handling $5T AUM and selling to HSBC and Janus Henderson, the name lacks authority.

**International viability:** Linguistically portable, but culturally shallow. No translation problems, but no resonance either. It will not mean anything to a PM in Singapore or a family office in Zurich.

**Institutional signal:** Weak. "Boosted.ai" does not belong in the same sentence as Bloomberg, FactSet, or MSCI. It reads like a growth-hacking tool or a fintech feature rather than a research infrastructure partner. Institutional buyers buy names that sound like they were built for serious people.

**DRI signal:** Accessible, but aspirational in the wrong direction. The Daily Reliant Investor wants to invest like a professional. "Boosted" sounds like a retail supplement brand. It does not signal edge, precision, or depth.

**Recommendation: Replace.** There is no scenario where "Boosted.ai" scales credibly across both institutional and consumer audiences. The name has served its purpose in a growth phase but carries no equity worth preserving into a category-defining position.

---

### "Alfa"

**Memorability and pronounceability:** Strong. Single syllable, distinctive spelling. The departure from the conventional "Alpha" spelling creates an immediate visual hook — it signals that something deliberate happened here. Globally unambiguous in pronunciation.

**Category fit:** Excellent, and arguably the best single word available to a finance AI product. "Alpha" is the foundational concept in investment management — the excess return above benchmark that every PM is paid to generate. Naming a research intelligence product "Alfa" is not metaphor; it is a direct statement of purpose. The company is not saying "we help you do your job." It is saying "we help you generate alpha." That is a fundamentally different claim.

**International viability:** Excellent. The concept of alpha travels across every investment culture globally. In fixed income, equities, alternatives, and macro — the word means the same thing to a quant in London as it does to an analyst in Toronto or Tokyo. The NATO phonetic alphabet association adds a layer of precision and authority that is free and automatic.

**Institutional signal:** High. "We use Alfa for earnings research" is a sentence that belongs in a Bloomberg user's vocabulary. The name invites comparison with serious infrastructure tools rather than fintech apps. Its main risk institutionally is the bar it sets — buyers may ask "does it actually deliver?" — which is a solvable marketing problem, not a naming problem.

**DRI signal:** Aspirational and accessible. The DRI who opens the app every trading day is an engaged, educated investor. Alpha is not an obscure term to this audience. If anything, the name is a promise: using this product is how serious investors behave. That is precisely the right tension to create.

**Risks:** Trademark complexity. Alfa Bank (Russia, though sanctions have reduced its global footprint), Alfa Romeo, and various smaller regional uses of "Alfa" exist. This requires a thorough trademark search. The more substantive risk is that "Alfa" as a product name is strong, but "Alfa" as a company name is unconventional — which is actually an argument for the elevation discussed in Section 3.

**Recommendation: Keep and elevate.** "Alfa" has more brand equity than "Boosted.ai" in every meaningful dimension. The pivot should consider making Alfa the company name, not replacing it.

## SECTION 2 — NAME EXPLORATION

### 1. VELA
**Category:** Short precision/action word

**Rationale:** Vela is the Latin word for sail, and the name of a constellation in the southern sky — historically used by navigators who could not rely on the North Star. It is a name for serious navigation. In finance, navigation metaphors resonate because markets are dynamic, uncertain, and require continuous course correction. The word is two syllables, clean, and aesthetically strong across Latin-based languages. "I check Vela every morning" is a natural habitual sentence for a DRI. "Our analysts use Vela for pre-earnings prep" works institutionally without needing explanation.

**Risks:** In Spanish, "vela" means candle — not problematic, but worth noting for Spanish-dominant markets. Some existing use in renewable energy (Vela Energy) and legal tech. Domain availability at vela.com is unlikely; vela.ai or brand-specific TLD would be needed.

**Domain likelihood:** Low for .com; moderate for alternatives.

---

### 2. FLETCHER
**Category:** Human advisor/partner name

**Rationale:** A fletcher was the craftsman who made arrows — the person who ensured your shot was accurate before you ever drew the bow. The name carries precision as its etymology and feels like a senior advisor at a respected firm rather than a software product. "Fletcher identified the risk before we did" is a sentence that works in an institutional sales call. The surname format gives it professional register without the stiffness of corporate acronyms. Compared to Harvey (legal AI), Fletcher occupies a parallel position in finance — a trusted expert who happens to run on infrastructure you rely on.

**Risks:** The name may skew too advisor-heavy for the Platform/developer product line. Engineers buying API access do not want to name a tool after a person. This works better for the Pro and consumer products than for the full company. fletcher.com almost certainly taken; fletcher.ai or a non-.ai TLD would be needed.

**Domain likelihood:** Moderate for brand-specific TLDs.

---

### 3. VERIS
**Category:** Invented/coined name

**Rationale:** Coined from the Latin root "verus" (true, real, genuine). The name signals truth and verification — the most underpromised and overneeded value in financial research. Research teams spend substantial effort validating data before they can trust it. "Veris says this is accurate" is a phrase that gets traction. The word does not exist in commerce in a major way, which means a cleaner trademark path than nearly any real word. It sounds invented but grounded — it rhymes with "Ferris," shares phonetics with "Paris," and reads as a credible institutional partner without being corporate or cold.

**Risks:** Latin root is not obvious to most users, so the meaning must be built by the brand rather than borrowed. Veris Residential is a public REIT; a trademark search would be required but the category distance is significant.

**Domain likelihood:** Moderate to high; veris.ai or veris.co likely available.

---

### 4. BASIS
**Category:** Financial/market concept reimagined

**Rationale:** Basis has multiple layers of meaning in finance: the difference between spot and futures price, the foundation of a trade, and the unit from which everything is measured (basis points). It signals foundational intelligence — not speculation, not noise, but the underlying fact that a decision is built on. "What's the basis?" is already a natural question on a trading floor. The name is short, confident, and without pretension. It does not try to be clever; it just is.

**Risks:** Highly generic; "basis" is used in company names across many industries (Basis Technologies in advertising). Trademark would be difficult to protect broadly. basis.com is almost certainly registered. The risk is you build a brand on a word you cannot own.

**Domain likelihood:** Very low for .com; moderate for category-specific TLDs.

---

### 5. TENOR
**Category:** Financial/market concept reimagined

**Rationale:** In fixed income and derivatives, tenor refers to the duration remaining until maturity — the time dimension of any position. The best investors think in tenor: they know not just whether they're right but when. The word also carries a musical meaning (the voice that carries the lead line) that reinforces the idea of intelligence running throughout a composition. Visually, it is clean. It has no negative connotations, travels internationally, and has no major direct competitors in finance or technology at scale.

**Risks:** Less immediately intuitive than some candidates — "tenor" is a fixed income term, and equity-focused PMs or consumer investors may not connect with it immediately. The musical association may dilute the finance signal unless the branding is deliberate. tenor.com is taken (Google's GIF platform).

**Domain likelihood:** Low for .com; moderate for alternatives.

---

### 6. MERIDIAN
**Category:** Unexpected conceptual

**Rationale:** The meridian is the moment of maximum clarity — solar noon, when the sun is at its highest point and navigation can be plotted with precision. In the age of celestial navigation, hitting the meridian was the most important moment of the day: the position fix everything downstream depended on. For a product whose core promise is giving investors clarity at the moment that matters, the metaphor is exact. The name has four syllables but reads with authority — it appears frequently in legal, medical, and professional services because it signals peak, precision, and credibility. It would be unusual in finance tech, which is a structural advantage.

**Risks:** Length. Four syllables is the outer limit for a strong brand name. "I use Meridian" works; "have you tried Meridian?" works less smoothly in casual conversation. Multiple existing companies called Meridian across industries would complicate trademark and cause early confusion. meridian.com is taken.

**Domain likelihood:** Low for .com; possible for industry-specific TLD.

---

### 7. ARCA
**Category:** Invented/coined with archival resonance

**Rationale:** From the Latin for treasury chest or strongbox — the container where the most important records and assets were kept. Short, invented-feeling without being meaningless, and carrying a specific signal: this is where financial intelligence is preserved and organized. The word works across languages without direct translation issues. It has no major incumbent in finance. The four-letter, two-syllable structure gives it the same typographic weight as Ramp or Kalshi — clean, modern, confident.

**Risks:** Arca is the name of a Venezuelan-American musical artist with a distinctive avant-garde identity. In finance and technology, this association is minor, but the name is not unclaimed. The ark/archive connotation could skew toward storage rather than intelligence if not positioned carefully.

**Domain likelihood:** Moderate; arca.com may be available or acquirable; arca.ai likely available.

---

### 8. QUILL
**Category:** Precision craft

**Rationale:** A quill was the instrument of precision record-keeping before the industrial era — the tool that separated the careful analysis from the rough ledger. It signals research, writing, craftsmanship, and editorial quality. For a product whose core function is intelligence distillation (40 hours of earnings prep to 20 minutes), the quill metaphor is apt. The word is one syllable, memorable, and unusual in technology. It shares the editorial, book-quality aesthetic of Harvey without being a name that requires explanation.

**Risks:** Quill.com is likely registered; Quill is a major office supply company in the US, which creates brand confusion in a country that is likely the primary consumer market. The association with handwriting and analog tools could also undercut the AI-forward identity.

**Domain likelihood:** Low for .com; quill.ai or quill.io possible.

---

### 9. KEEL
**Category:** Unexpected structural metaphor

**Rationale:** The keel is the structural spine of a ship — the single component that provides directional stability in rough water. Without it, a vessel cannot maintain course. For investors navigating market volatility, "keel" signals exactly what an AI research platform should provide: not speculation, not excitement, but structural stability and direction. The name is one syllable, short, visually clean, and has no direct competitors in finance or technology. It is surprising without being obscure.

**Risks:** "Keel" can also mean to "keel over" — collapse, capsize — which is an obvious risk for a finance brand. The negative connotation is real and persistent. Brand teams would need to work against it. keel.com status is uncertain; it may be available.

**Domain likelihood:** Moderate; worth investigating.

---

### 10. CROFT
**Category:** Unexpected old-money precision

**Rationale:** A croft is an enclosed, cultivated field — private, managed, and productive. The word carries old-world weight without being archaic. It signals a particular kind of careful, bounded, intentional management that is exactly the psychological register the DRI and institutional buyer share: they both want someone (or something) that takes their financial life seriously and handles it with craft. The name is one syllable, typographically strong, and completely unclaimed in finance. It sits aesthetically between Harvey and Ramp — human warmth with precision.

**Risks:** Lara Croft is a globally recognized pop culture character; some brand teams would see this as a distraction. The connotation of a small farm may feel quaint rather than confident to institutional buyers without careful positioning. croft.com likely unavailable.

**Domain likelihood:** Low for .com; croft.ai may be available.

## SECTION 3 — ARCHITECTURE RECOMMENDATION

**Recommendation: Endorsed master brand, with Alfa elevated to company name.**

The three structural options — single brand (monolithic), house of brands, and endorsed architecture — each carry different trade-offs, and the right choice flows directly from Boosted.ai's specific situation.

**Why not house of brands:** A house of brands (Procter & Gamble, Unilever) requires marketing budget at scale for each independent brand. Boosted.ai has 300 institutional clients, a consumer product in development, and a developer API. Running three entirely separate brand identities requires three separate brand-building investments. That is not the right resource allocation for a company at this stage.

**Why not purely monolithic (single brand):** The risk with a single brand serving both "Alfa Pro" (institutional PM research tool) and a consumer app is that each audience senses the product was designed for the other. The PM doing earnings research does not want to use an app that looks like it was built for retail investors. The DRI does not want to use something that feels like a Bloomberg terminal that has been simplified for them. Some degree of product differentiation is necessary to serve both audiences authentically.

**Why endorsed master brand:** An endorsed architecture resolves both problems. The company has one name — and that name carries the trust, credibility, and institutional relationships that took 8 years to build. Each product carries a descriptor that signals its specific audience and use case. The master brand lends its credibility to each product; each product does not have to build brand equity from zero.

**The specific recommendation:** Elevate "Alfa" to the company name.

Alfa is the strongest naming asset the company currently owns. It has been in market with 300 institutional clients. It speaks the language of finance natively. It carries a claim — alpha generation — that is both aspirational for DRI and technically credible for institutional buyers. "Boosted.ai" has none of these attributes.

The resulting architecture:

- **Alfa** — company name and parent brand
- **Alfa Pro** — institutional research tool (PMs, analysts at hedge funds and asset managers)
- **Alfa Platform** — developer infrastructure (quants, engineers, API + MCP)
- **Alfa** — consumer product (unmodified, the flagship; this is the naming move that signals consumer is the future)

This structure has a precise logic: the consumer product carries the parent name unmodified because it is the primary product going forward. The institutional and developer products carry modifiers because they are specialized applications of the core intelligence. When a DRI searches "Alfa," they find the consumer product. When an institutional buyer searches "Alfa Pro," they find their product. The brand is one; the products are differentiated by suffix.

If Alfa faces insurmountable trademark obstacles at the company level, the fallback architecture is: **New Company Name (e.g., Vela)** as the parent brand, with the Alfa product family retained underneath — Alfa Pro, Alfa Platform, Alfa. This preserves all institutional product equity while giving the company a clean new identity at the corporate level.

## SECTION 4 — FINAL RECOMMENDATIONS

### TOP RECOMMENDATION: ALFA (elevated to company name)

**Core rationale:** The principle in naming strategy is to preserve equity wherever it exists and replace it only where it creates active harm. "Boosted.ai" creates active harm at every level — it undermines credibility with institutional buyers, fails to signal aspiration for DRI consumers, and is architecturally dead in a post-".ai" suffix market. "Alfa" creates no harm anywhere. It has eight years of institutional relationship equity, a natural linguistic claim in the most fundamental concept of investment management, and a visual distinctiveness (the spelling) that works as a brand asset.

The question of whether to keep "Alfa" or find a new name should be answered as follows: if you replace Alfa, what do you gain that you do not already have? The answer is close to nothing. Alfa is already short, clean, globally pronounceable, and finance-native. The rebrand energy should be spent on the company name (replacing Boosted.ai) and the visual identity — not on replacing the product name that 300 institutional clients already trust.

**How it works for institutional buyers:** "Alfa Pro" signals a professional-grade research tool built by a company whose name is alpha. Every institutional PM knows the word. The claim is implicit in the name: this product helps you generate alpha. That is not a feature statement; it is a product category statement. It belongs next to Bloomberg, FactSet, and Refinitiv in a slide deck and does not look out of place.

**How it works for DRI:** The Daily Reliant Investor aspires to invest with the same rigor as a professional. Alfa is the signal of professional finance. The DRI who opens the app every trading day wants to feel that they are doing what a hedge fund analyst does. "I use Alfa" is a sentence a serious individual investor says with quiet confidence. It does not shout; it does not explain itself. That is exactly right.

**Product pairing:** Alfa (company) + Alfa Pro + Alfa Platform + Alfa (consumer flagship). Clean, logical, scalable.

**Primary risk to mitigate:** Trademark. The Alfa name requires a thorough international trademark search with particular attention to financial services. The most credible challenge comes from Eastern European banking (Alfa Bank and affiliated entities). If trademark counsel identifies a clearable path — which is likely given that none of the incumbent Alfa trademark holders operate a finance AI research platform — proceed. If not, Vela is the recommended fallback.

---

### SECOND RECOMMENDATION: VELA (new company name, if Alfa faces trademark barriers)

**Core rationale:** Among genuinely new names, Vela has the strongest combination of: short form (two syllables), clean phonetics that work across all major languages, a metaphor that earns its meaning (navigation, stars, the southern sky that required expertise to read), and no negative connotations anywhere. It reads as premium without being cold. It is unusual in finance without being obscure. The visual identity possibilities — celestial, nautical, directional — give designers genuine latitude without defaulting to finance clichés.

**How it works for institutional buyers:** "Our analysts use Vela for earnings research" is a credible sentence. The name does not make a claim that invites skepticism; it simply exists with confidence. This is actually useful in institutional sales, where buyers are allergic to products that oversell. Vela says nothing and everything simultaneously — which, at the enterprise level, is often a feature.

**How it works for DRI:** Navigation metaphors resonate with active investors. The DRI corrects course constantly — changing positions, re-evaluating theses, tracking news. "Vela helps me navigate the market" is a natural locution. The name also supports a "daily check" habit better than many candidates: navigation requires regular position fixes, which mirrors the DRI's daily opening behavior.

**Product pairing:** Vela (company) + Vela Pro + Vela Platform + Vela (consumer). Or, if Alfa product equity is preserved: Vela (company) + Alfa Pro + Alfa Platform + Alfa (consumer), using the endorsed architecture where the company name and product family are distinct.

**Primary risk to mitigate:** The split architecture (Vela company, Alfa products) creates a two-name brand family that requires explanation. This is manageable but adds marketing complexity. If Vela is chosen as the company name, the cleaner path is to rename the products to the Vela family and accept the transition cost, rather than running two brand names simultaneously.

---

### THIRD RECOMMENDATION: VERIS (for a fully clean break with maximum trademark optionality)

**Core rationale:** If the decision is made to replace both Boosted.ai and Alfa — a complete rebrand with no equity preserved from either — Veris is the strongest candidate. It is coined rather than borrowed, which means the trademark path is the cleanest possible. It carries real semantic content (Latin: "verus," true/real/genuine) without requiring that content to be explained, because the word sounds credible on its own. In a market where AI product names are increasingly distrusted because of hallucinations and unreliable outputs, a name that signals veracity is positioning as much as branding.

**How it works for institutional buyers:** Institutional buyers are deeply concerned with data accuracy. The name "Veris" — even without explaining the etymology — creates an implicit promise of reliability and verification. "Our research is Veris-verified" is a locution that could develop naturally. The name has the right sonic register for a platform that sits next to Bloomberg: serious, grounded, international.

**How it works for DRI:** The DRI who checks the app every trading day needs to trust what they are seeing. Veris signals that the information is real — not curated, not biased, not shaped by incentive. In a market full of apps with undisclosed conflicts of interest, truth as a brand attribute is differentiated. "I trust Veris" is a more resonant consumer claim than "I use Veris."

**Product pairing:** Veris (company) + Veris Pro + Veris Platform + Veris (consumer). Or with a sub-brand personality shift for the consumer product: Veris (company) + Pro/Platform for institutional/developer + a consumer-specific product name that is more personal and daily-habit-oriented.

**Primary risk to mitigate:** A fully invented name carries no borrowed equity. Every brand association has to be built from scratch — which is expensive in time and marketing spend. This is the right choice if the company wants maximum creative freedom and the cleanest possible break from the past. It is not the right choice if any of the institutional relationships built under "Alfa" are worth preserving in name recognition.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Replace "Boosted.ai." It has no equity worth preserving and actively undermines the brand at both ends of the audience spectrum. Keep and elevate "Alfa" to the company name — it is the strongest naming asset the company owns, it speaks finance natively, and it provides a natural architecture for Pro, Platform, and consumer products. Adopt an endorsed master brand structure with Alfa as the parent. If Alfa faces trademark barriers, Vela is the cleanest fallback for the company name while preserving the Alfa product family. If a fully clean break is required, Veris offers the best combination of semantic credibility and trademark optionality among fully invented names.
