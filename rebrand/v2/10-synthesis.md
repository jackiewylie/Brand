## DOCUMENT 1: WHAT CHANGED AND WHY
### For the CMO

---

### What V1 Got Wrong

**1. It kept the wrong color doing the wrong job.**
V1 retained the gold (#C19A6B) from the hedge fund sales brand. Gold in financial services is a prestige signal aimed at institutional LPs — it says "we have been here a long time and charge accordingly." For the DRI, gold reads as aspirational-luxury fintech, which is exactly the category Boosted.ai is not trying to occupy. The CMO cited Kalshi and Ramp as reference points: neither uses gold. Both use a single strong color with a specific functional job. V1 kept a color that solved a problem for a different audience and a different competitive moment.

**2. It treated the dark-ground as fixed.**
V1 applied the existing near-black surface without questioning whether it was the right ground for the product's next chapter. Dark editorial works when you own the execution at Harvey's level — extreme typographic precision, book-quality composition, a design team with a clear mandate. As a default, it produces a product that looks like a premium newsletter competitor. The parchment ground in V2 is not warm for warmth's sake: it creates native document familiarity for institutional buyers (who live in filings and research reports) while being completely novel in the consumer fintech landscape. The ground does strategic work that near-black cannot.

**3. It had no visual mechanism for AI trust.**
V1 produced no answer to the central trust problem in AI-generated financial content: how does a user know what the model said versus what the editorial context says? In a financial product, that separation is not a design preference — it is a compliance and trust obligation. V2 answers this with the stamp: a violet-bordered element that appears exclusively on model-generated outputs. Every other surface is stamp-free. The visual rule creates a categorical separation that V1 never attempted.

**4. It did not resolve the naming architecture.**
V1 operated inside the Boosted.ai brand without questioning whether the company name itself was the right vehicle for a consumer pivot. "Boosted.ai" is a startup name built for a B2B fundraising context. It does not survive a DRI-facing App Store listing. V2 addresses this at the entity level: Alfa becomes the master brand, Boosted.ai is retired, and the naming system scales cleanly from consumer flagship to institutional suite without requiring explanation.

---

### What V2 Chose and Why

**Selected territory: The Official Record**

The brand looks and feels like a financial document that has been elevated — not designed in the trendy sense, but built with the density and architectural precision of a primary source. The ground is parchment (#E8E0CE): warm, matte, institutional, nothing like any current fintech competitor. The typeface is a single condensed grotesque (Barlow Condensed) used across all scales, all weights, all surfaces — no serifs, no italics, no secondary family. The result is a system that reads as constructed rather than styled. It has the visual authority of a prospectus and the specificity of something that could not have been designed without knowing exactly what it is for.

The signature gesture is the stamp: a rectangular violet-bordered block that marks AI-generated outputs. It is the only place violet appears in the system. Violet is not decorative — it is categorical. When you see the border, the model generated what is inside it. When you do not see the border, it did not. That is the entire rule.

The verbal register is declarative and spare. Short sentences. Closed claims. Financial vocabulary used precisely because it is precise, not because it signals sophistication. The brand does not aspire on behalf of the user — it meets them at the level of seriousness they already occupy.

**Naming: Alfa as master brand, Boosted.ai retired**

Alfa carries eight years of institutional relationship equity. Three hundred clients at $5T AUM know the word. The spelling signals deliberateness: Alfa, not Alpha, is the NATO phonetic designation — the first signal sent when clarity is required. That is the company. The consumer product takes the unmodified name (Alfa) because the strategic claim is structural: the most important product in the company's future gets the parent brand's name directly. Alfa Pro serves the institutional PM and analyst market. Alfa Platform serves the quant and developer infrastructure buyer. The architecture is clean, the suffix logic is descriptive rather than aspirational, and the system scales without explanation.

**Brand platform shift from V1**

V1 positioned around performance — AI that makes your research better. V2 positions around access and provenance. The performance claims are present but subordinated to two more defensible ideas: that institutional research infrastructure was always good enough, it was just never distributed to the people who could use it; and that AI earns trust in financial research through accountability for its outputs, not through capability claims. "The record, officially" is not a tagline about what Alfa does — it is a statement about what Alfa is. A certified, stamped, traceable record of what serious financial analysis produces. That is a category definition, not a feature description. V1 did not attempt a category definition.

### The Key Strategic Bets

**1. Parchment as category-defining ground.**
No financial product at scale — not Bloomberg, not Robinhood, not Public.com, not any Harvey-adjacent wealth management rebrand — occupies parchment. If this execution is disciplined, the ground itself becomes a brand asset. The bet: the competitive landscape's preference for dark editorial or clinical white leaves parchment uncontested, and the institutional document associations work in Alfa's favor with both audiences.

**2. One typeface family as operational discipline.**
Single-family condensed grotesque is a constraint that most brand teams will not hold through eighteen months of product development. The bet is that the constraint is the system: a single typeface executed at maximum compression across eight weight variations and a wide type scale is more visually distinctive than a dual-family system executed loosely. The difficulty of maintaining it is the competitive moat.

**3. Violet doing one job and one job only.**
Violet appears exclusively on AI-generated outputs. The bet is that this semantic clarity is itself a product differentiator and a trust argument — that investors will come to associate the violet stamp with accountability for model outputs in a way that no competitor has built into their visual language. The risk is that the rule gets violated in product development; the rule must be treated as non-negotiable for the bet to pay off.

**4. Consumer = primary, institutional = credentialing.**
Giving the unmodified Alfa name to the consumer product is a public strategic bet. It says: the consumer business is where this company is going. Institutional credibility (300 clients, $5T AUM, HSBC and Janus Henderson and Invesco) functions as social proof for the DRI, not as the primary revenue narrative. The bet is that the DRI is a large enough market with enough purchase intent to justify making consumer the flagship claim.

---

### Risks and Trade-offs

**Violet adjacency with productivity tools.**
Notion, Figma, and Loom all use purple-adjacent primaries. At the wrong execution register — rounded corners, clean white backgrounds, friendly copy — the brand reads as a productivity startup rather than a financial institution. The risk is neutralized by context (parchment ground, condensed type, rectilinear forms, financial vocabulary) but requires that the execution rules hold. A product team that rounds the buttons, lightens the parchment, or uses the brand color decoratively collapses the argument.

**Parchment legibility in dark mode.**
The dark mode inversion is warm near-black with parchment migrating to text — this is structurally sound but requires color validation across WCAG contrast thresholds on every surface combination. It will require more QA than a standard dark mode implementation because the warm undertones must be preserved precisely or the system reads as muddy.

**The institutional sales team's comfort.**
The current brand was built for hedge fund sales. Some of the existing sales team will experience the parchment-and-violet system as a step away from the authority register they have used for eight years. This is a change management problem, not a brand problem — but it requires that the CMO arms the sales team with the institutional trust argument (parchment-as-document-register, stamp-as-provenance, condensed type-as-architectural) before the first client pitch under the new system.

**What this direction gives up.**
It gives up the warm editorial warmth that Territory 2 offered and that the Harvey reference brand deploys effectively. It gives up the immediate approachability of a friendly design system. It does not give up accessibility — but it demands that "accessible" be understood as "serious people deserve serious tools," not "here is a gentle onboarding experience." The DRI who wants a supportive, encouraging product relationship will not find that here. That is the correct trade.

---

### If You Do Nothing

Boosted.ai in its current form has a naming problem, a consumer-entry problem, and a trust-architecture problem that will compound in the next twelve months.

The naming problem: "Boosted.ai" cannot launch in the App Store as a consumer flagship. Any consumer marketing that runs under this name will require explanation that the product name should not require. The DRI does not look for their research tool by searching for "boosted." They search for "Alfa" because that is what their institutional contacts already call it.

The consumer-entry problem: the current brand was built for a different company. Every element — the dark ground, the gold, the light-weight grotesque — was optimized for hedge fund sales credibility. A consumer product launched under this visual system will be read as a premium institutional tool that has been reluctantly democratized, rather than as research infrastructure that was always meant to reach this audience. The DRI is sophisticated enough to read that signal and respond to it negatively.

The trust-architecture problem: every AI financial product launched in the next twenty-four months will face regulatory and user-trust scrutiny around model-generated outputs. Without a visual system that separates AI outputs from editorial context at the design layer, Alfa will be forced to solve this problem through UI chrome (disclaimers, footnotes, modal explanations) that degrades the experience and signals defensiveness. The stamp solves this problem at the brand layer before it becomes a compliance problem.

The competitive window for "institutional-grade AI research, available to individuals" is open now. It will not remain open. If Boosted.ai does not occupy this position with a coherent brand under the Alfa name, a well-funded competitor will. The current brand is not a neutral holding position — it is a cost that increases with every quarter it remains.

**What we are recommending and what it looks like.**

Retire Boosted.ai at the corporate entity level. The company is now Alfa. The consumer product is Alfa, unmodified. Institutional products are Alfa Pro (PM and analyst research tool) and Alfa Platform (API and developer infrastructure). No other names in the family.

The visual system is built on parchment — a warm off-white document ground (#E8E0CE) that no current competitor occupies. One typeface (Barlow Condensed), compressed and architectural. One brand color (electric violet, #4718DC) with a single job: violet borders mark AI-generated outputs. Everything else is ink on parchment. The brand mark is "ALFA" in Barlow Condensed Black, set full-width at hero scale. There is no logomark. The word is the mark.

This is what it looks like: a financial primary source that has never existed before. Not a newsletter. Not a fintech app. Not a Bloomberg terminal modernized. Something new that reads immediately as authoritative and immediately as different from every vendor in the space.

---

**Core strategic logic.**

Institutional research infrastructure was always good enough. It was a distribution problem, not a capability problem. Alfa solves the distribution problem — and the brand needs to say that clearly rather than hiding it inside performance claims about AI.

The stamp is the trust mechanism. In the next twenty-four months, every AI financial product will face user-trust and regulatory scrutiny over model-generated outputs. The stamp — a violet-bordered block that appears exclusively on Alfa-generated content — solves this problem at the brand layer. Visual provenance is built in from day one. Competitors will have to retrofit it. We build it into the mark.

The unmodified consumer name is the strategic signal. Giving "Alfa" to the consumer product, not to a tier, is the company saying publicly: this is where we are going.

---

**What Studio Freight adds over this AI work.**

This output covers brand strategy, verbal identity, naming architecture, visual system logic, CSS execution brief, and copy platform — at a level of rigor that is production-ready in direction. What it does not do: motion systems, production-quality logotype refinement, asset library build-out, icon system, product component library, pitch deck templates, print and merchandise standards, and the execution review that catches the ten decisions in the first product sprint where the brand system gets quietly diluted.

The honest answer: if the product and growth teams are disciplined about holding the rules, you can launch on this without Studio Freight. The risk is not that this direction is wrong — it is that implementation without a design partner tends to produce "close enough" decisions in product development that, compounded over six months, collapse the system's visual specificity. Parchment becomes off-white. Violet becomes decorative. The stamp gets applied to editorial content. The condensed type gets supplemented with a "more readable" body face. Each individual decision seems minor. Together they produce a brand that has the right idea and the wrong execution. Studio Freight's value is not ideation at this stage — it is holding the line.

---

**The decision.**

There are three options.

**Option A:** Proceed on this direction internally. Brand strategy and verbal identity are final. Assign a product designer who understands the rules to own the execution. Run Studio Freight as an audit at six months rather than a build partner from the start.

**Option B:** Take this direction to Studio Freight as a finished brief. They are not starting from scratch — they are executing against a complete strategy. The conversation changes: instead of "help us figure out who we are," it is "here is exactly who we are, build the production system." That is a faster and cheaper engagement than a full brand discovery process.

**Option C:** Do nothing. The analysis above covers what this costs. The consumer launch window is the forcing function. Boosted.ai cannot be the name on the App Store listing. That decision has a deadline whether or not the rebrand does.

The recommendation is Option B. The strategy is done. The execution requires a production partner. Schedule the Studio Freight conversation with this brief, not a blank canvas.
