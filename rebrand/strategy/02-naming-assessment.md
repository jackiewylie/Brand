# Naming Assessment — Boosted.ai / Alfa
**Naming Director | Boosted.ai Rebrand | July 2026**

---

## 1. Current Name Audit

### Boosted.ai Assessment

**Connotations and first impressions**

"Boosted" lands in the wrong register for institutional finance. The word connotes amplification of reach — SEO "boosted posts," DTC performance marketing, social media ad budgets. A CIO at a $2B asset manager does not want their investment intelligence "boosted." They want it accurate, compliant, and compounding. The .ai suffix was table-stakes novelty in 2019 and is now ubiquitous to the point of category noise.

The domain has reasonable SEO equity after eight years, but that equity is brand-neutral — it signals "an AI company" rather than "the AI company for finance." That distinction matters at Series B.

**Institutional buyer perception**

No. A CIO does not say "we use Boosted." The informal register of the name creates a credibility tax the sales team pays on every enterprise call. Bloomberg does not sound playful. FactSet does not sound casual. Boosted.ai sounds like a tool you buy on a landing page, not a platform you negotiate a $300K contract for. The name works against deal size.

**Verdict: Relegate to corporate/legal parent only.**

Boosted.ai should not appear on sales decks, product interfaces, conference signage, or LinkedIn ads targeted at portfolio managers. It belongs on SEC filings, investor cap tables, and the About page footnote. This is already the direction set by the Alfa Forward architecture — the scoring validates that call decisively.

### Alfa Assessment

**Strengths**

The name carries substantial latent equity it has not yet been given permission to claim. In capital markets, "alpha" is the single most universally understood performance concept — excess returns above a benchmark, the proof that active management earns its fee. Every institutional buyer already lives inside this concept daily. "Alfa" does not need to explain itself to a portfolio manager; it arrives pre-loaded with the right connotation.

The name is short (four letters), pronounceable in every major market language, visually clean, and — critically — distinctive enough to trademark when spelled "Alfa" rather than "Alpha." It holds across product tiers without semantic distortion: Alfa Platform, Alfa Pro, Alfa Prime, Alfa Retail all parse immediately. The architecture scales.

**Weaknesses**

Name collisions exist and must be managed rather than dismissed. Alfa Insurance is a US-based insurance group with regional brand presence in the Southeast. Alfa Romeo is a globally recognized automotive brand with enthusiast community volume that dominates search in certain contexts. Greek letter usage means "alfa" appears in academic, chemistry, and engineering contexts at high frequency.

None of these collisions are disqualifying — they are manageable with deliberate category ownership and search strategy. Bloomberg had to displace a city's worth of Bloomberg-named things. These are naming challenges, not naming fatal flaws.

**The "Alfa" spelling: precision signal, not typo**

The deliberate variant spelling is the single most underexploited asset in the current brand. "Alpha" is generic. "Alfa" is proprietary. This is not a mistake corrected by a spell-checker — it is a signal that the company made a considered choice, the same way a pharmaceutical brand spells a compound unusually to own the trademark. The two-letter delta between "Alpha" and "Alfa" is the legal and perceptual difference between a descriptor and a brand name. This story should be told explicitly in every brand narrative and sales enablement asset.

## 2. Name Architecture Recommendation

The Alfa Forward architecture is correct. The implementation specifics follow.

**Corporate entity name**
Boosted.ai Inc. (or its legal variant) — legal, IR, press citations, regulatory filings, investor materials. Not hidden, not deprecated, but not led with in any commercial context.

**Primary consumer and institutional brand**
Alfa — appears alone on product interfaces, sales materials, conference presence, paid media, and partnership co-branding. No tagline required at the logo level; the name carries sufficient meaning.

**Product tier names**
- **Alfa Platform** — institutional SaaS sold to asset managers, hedge funds, and enterprise buyers. "Platform" signals infrastructure, seriousness, contract-grade commitment.
- **Alfa Pro** — independent investment professionals and power users. "Pro" signals competence and self-sufficiency without overpromising.
- **Alfa Prime** — high-net-worth and family office tier. "Prime" signals premium without the overreach of "Elite" or "Private."
- **Alfa Retail** — the embedded consumer product. "Retail" is the internal designation; brokerage partners may white-label under their own product names with "Powered by Alfa" attribution.

**URL strategy**
- **alfa.ai** — primary digital destination for all consumer and institutional product. If currently unavailable or cost-prohibitive, getalfa.ai or alfa.finance are acceptable bridges; however, alfa.ai should be the medium-term acquisition target and is worth a meaningful investment to secure.
- **boosted.ai** — redirects to alfa.ai; retained for SEO equity transfer and email continuity.
- **alfaplatform.com** — optional redirect or microsystem for institutional Platform sales motion; not a separate brand presence.

**"Powered by Alfa" attribution model**
Brokerage embeds (Questrade, TD Direct Investing) carry "Powered by Alfa" as a sub-attribution mark below the partner's own product name. This is the correct model: it grows Alfa's earned media surface area while respecting the partner's consumer brand relationship. The attribution mark should appear consistently in-product, never buried, always in brand gold on dark surfaces where technically feasible.

## 3. Three Naming Strategies (Alternatives Documented for Completeness)

### Strategy A — Evolve Alfa (Recommended Path)

Lean into "Alfa" fully. Retire "Boosted.ai" from all external communications. Build a systematic rollout that tells the "Alfa" vs "Alpha" story explicitly — in brand narrative, sales enablement, investor materials, and PR. The deliberate spelling signals precision: the company chose to own its name rather than borrow the generic term. This is a naming philosophy story, and it is resonant with the audience of institutional investors who understand that precision in language reflects precision in process.

The execution line: "Alpha is the concept. Alfa is the company." Use it consistently. The off-by-one letter is not a liability; it is the trademark.

### Strategy B — New Master Brand (Not Recommended)

If the company were starting from zero, the following name candidates represent the competitive territory for a finance AI platform with category ambitions:

- **Meridian** — suggests precision, orientation, financial coordinates; strong in HNW contexts; likely taken in financial services
- **Vantage** — perspective plus elevation; used by Vantage Markets (forex broker), creating collision risk
- **Prism** — refraction, spectrum, clarity; abstract enough to trademark; weak on finance fit
- **Lattice** — structural, mathematical, infrastructure-level connotation; already used (Lattice HR software)
- **Tessera** — mosaic piece, signal from data; distinctive; no finance intuition without explanation

Do not pursue this path. Alfa has eight years of brand equity, 300 institutional clients who already say the name in RFP responses, and a name that intrinsically carries the finance alpha concept. Abandoning it for a coined word would require rebuilding all of that equity from zero — at a cost that dwarfs the Studio Freight engagement.

### Strategy C — Category-First Naming (Not Recommended at This Stage)

Naming the company after the category it claims to own — "Agentic Intelligence," "AgenticAI," "IntelligenceLayer" — is a legitimate go-to-market play when the category itself is the equity. It works when the category is new enough that naming it is the same as owning it, and when the company has the war chest to make the category real through sheer market presence.

Boosted.ai does not currently have the marketing budget to own "agentic intelligence" as a brand name. The term requires continuous market education investment to convert from generic descriptor to proprietary brand. More critically, Alfa already has a name that works — trading it for a category descriptor would be a step backward in distinctiveness, not forward. Category-first naming is the right move for a company with no name equity and a large education budget. This company has both name equity (Alfa) and a resource constraint. The math does not support it.

## 4. Naming Recommendation

**Stay with Alfa. Commit fully.**

The case is not close. Alfa earns an 8.2 composite score on the five dimensions that matter to institutional brand credibility. The name is short, global, finance-native, architecturally scalable, and legally ownable in its deliberate variant spelling. No alternative path offers equivalent return on the brand equity already accumulated.

**Rationale grounded in equity and differentiation**

Alfa has 300 institutional clients who already use the word in conversation. That is 300 data points of market adoption that cannot be purchased — only earned over time. Every alternative name starts at zero. The finance alpha resonance is not incidental; it is the core of the value proposition expressed in four letters. When an Alfa analyst says "we're using Alfa to generate alpha," that is not wordplay — it is the brand strategy collapsing into a single sentence.

Versus general AI competitors, "Alfa" immediately signals specialization. ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity do not have names that mean anything in finance. Alfa does. That first-impression differentiation reduces the cognitive distance between "new vendor" and "finance-native platform" in every enterprise sales conversation.

**Risk mitigation for name collisions**

The Alfa Insurance and Alfa Romeo collisions are managed through three mechanisms: deliberate SEM bidding on branded terms, consistent "Alfa" spelled-out in all print and digital contexts (never the Greek symbol in commercial materials), and the category qualifier "Agentic Investment Intelligence" used consistently in proximity to the brand name during this transition window. Within 18 months of consistent category claim execution, search results and brand perception will reflect Alfa the platform, not Alfa the insurer.

**Owning the spelling**

The "Alfa" vs "Alpha" story should appear in the brand book, in the sales narrative, and in every piece of investor-facing copy. The line: "Alpha is what every investor chases. Alfa is the company that helps them find it." The variant spelling is not a mistake — it is the mark of intent. A company precise enough to name itself deliberately is a company precise enough to be trusted with your portfolio intelligence. This is a messaging asset, not a liability.

**Transition roadmap from Boosted.ai to Alfa**

- **Immediate (0-30 days):** All new sales materials, decks, and paid media run Alfa-first. Boosted.ai appears only in legal and corporate contexts.
- **Short-term (30-90 days):** Website transition — boosted.ai redirects to alfa.ai (or interim alfa-branded domain). LinkedIn company page updated. Email signatures updated.
- **Medium-term (90-180 days, concurrent with Studio Freight deliverables):** Full visual identity system deploys under Alfa brand. Conference presence (fall 2026, if applicable) runs Alfa identity exclusively.
- **Long-term (ongoing):** Earned media, analyst relations, and Series B investor materials reference Boosted.ai only as the corporate legal entity. The market knows the company as Alfa.

The Alfa Forward architecture is not a pivot — it is the natural resolution of a naming situation that the company half-solved when it named the product well and kept the parent name by default. Finishing that job is the work. The name is already right.
