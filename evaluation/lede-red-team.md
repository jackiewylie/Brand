# Red team: is "Lede" actually the best possible brand for Boosted?

*Post-delivery adversarial review, October 2026 (working exercise). Method: attack the chosen name harder than the engagement did, mine two territories the funnel under-explored, and either replace Lede or confirm it with priced risks and trigger conditions.*

## Attack 1 — the homophone tax is real and was under-priced

Spoken aloud, "Lede" = "lead," and *lead* is an active word in this exact category (lead investor, sales leads, "lead position"). Word-of-mouth — a real acquisition channel for a product people trust with money — degrades: "you should try Lede" → hears "Lead" → searches "lead app" → finds a CRM. The delivery book resolved this in one line ("rhymes with feed"), which handles *pronunciation*, not *transcription*.

**Pricing it honestly:** this is a measurable, bounded tax, not a fatal flaw. Mitigations: (a) always show the wordmark when the name is said (video, podcast reads scripted as "Lede — L-E-D-E"), (b) own the misspelling surface: buy `getlead.app`-class typo domains and brand-search terms for "lead investing app" from day one, (c) the tagline does disambiguation work ("Don't bury the lede" teaches the spelling in the same breath as the meaning). Verdict: survivable, and — critically — every finalist alternative has its own tax of similar size (below).

## Attack 2 — the LedeAI adjacency is closer than the book framed

LedeAI (automated local-news writing) is not just "another class" — both companies are *AI systems that produce news-like output*. A confusion argument in class 42 is at least arguable. **This is the single hardest risk on the name**, and it is binary: counsel either clears it or doesn't.

**Consequence:** the naming decision must be written as conditional, not absolute. Lede proceeds to filing; if opposition risk is rated high, the bench activates. That is what benches are for. (The bench also gets a second name — see Attack 4.)

## Attack 3 — does a journalism metaphor undersell computation?

The product's deepest differentiator is *computed, auditable numbers* — not editorial judgment. Does "Lede" frame Boosted as a news product? Tested against the actual promise: the lede is not "news" — it is *ranked materiality*, the discipline of putting what matters first. That is precisely the product's one job. And journalism's idiom of verification ("show your sources") is the consumer-legible version of institutional auditability. The metaphor holds; in fact no other territory found gives verification and priority in one word. Attack fails.

## Attack 4 — the under-mined territories, mined

Two territories the funnel passed too quickly, now worked to exhaustion:

**"The person you'd hire" (staff-for-the-staffless):** Adjutant, Steward, Second, Deskmate, Chief. All fail test 2 or 4 — either servile (butler energy the strategy explicitly rejected), obscure-military, or unbrandable as a master brand. Territory closed.

**"Finance-native leading signals":** the standout is **Bellwether** — *the* investor's word for the stock that signals the market's direction. Semantically superb: first-to-know, native to the buyer's own vocabulary, serious and warm. Taxes: three syllables; chronic misspelling ("bellweather" — a worse spelling tax than Lede's homophone, because users type it, not just say it); moderately crowded mark; and no product lexicon (a flagged item can't be "a bellwether" — the word means the *stock*, not the *alert*). Scored against the seven tests it beats Standfirst on meaning but loses to Lede on speakability, lexicon, and length. **Action taken: Bellwether replaces Masthead as bench #2**, behind Standfirst.

## Re-scored finalists (post-red-team)

| Test (weight) | Lede | Standfirst | Bellwether |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entity-clean | Strong (distinct spelling) | Strong | Moderate |
| Carries the job | **Perfect** — meaning *is* the promise | Strong | Strong (signal, not priority) |
| Speaks well | Homophone tax, mitigable | Clean but jargon-obscure (UK newsroom term unknown to US buyers — a *meaning* tax larger than Lede's spelling tax, since "bury the lede" is common American idiom and "standfirst" is not) | Misspelling tax, 3 syllables |
| Product lexicon | **"a lede," The Morning Lede** — builds itself | Weak ("a standfirst" = nothing to US users) | None |
| Clearable | Conditional — LedeAI adjacency is the live risk | Likely cleanest | Moderate |
| Master brand | Strong | Mouthful in lockups | Long in lockups |

## Verdict — conviction, with tripwires

**Lede stands.** It is the only candidate where the name, the tagline, the product lexicon, the daily ritual, and the verification story are one system — every alternative wins a single row and loses the system. The two real risks are priced and tripwired:

1. **Legal tripwire:** if counsel rates the LedeAI/class-42 opposition risk high → activate **Standfirst** (bench #1), full identity system ports with a wordmark re-cut (the Masthead & the Mark is name-agnostic by design — highlight gesture, paper/ink/mark palette, and type system all survive).
2. **Market tripwire:** if 90-day post-launch data shows word-of-mouth misattribution above threshold (branded-search capture of "lead app" variants failing despite mitigation spend) → escalate spelling-forward creative (the wordmark *is* the ad) before considering anything drastic.

The final proof is not another memo — it is whether the brand carries a real hero surface. See `launch/index.html`: the lede.com concept homepage, built in the delivered system. If that page works, the brand works.
