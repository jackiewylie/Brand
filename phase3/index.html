Why Vigil over Brief

Brief is the strongest option on phonetic simplicity — one syllable, zero ambiguity, "Your Briefs" is a completely natural plural. The rejection is not on those grounds. Brief positions the platform as a reporting output — the document the instrument produces — rather than as the monitoring system itself. For Territory A's instrument argument, a name that describes the sustained action (the vigil maintained between events, continuously, across 47 names) is more architecturally coherent than a name that describes the artifact produced at an event endpoint (the brief). The decisive criterion is D5·3: "Ask [Name]" for the agent interface. "Ask Brief a question" is syntactically awkward in a way that "Ask Vigil a question" is not. This is a small friction, but it is documented in Phase 2 ("Ask [Name] should work for the agent interface" — the criterion is implicitly harder to meet for "Brief"), and it compounds across every user interaction with the platform's agent surface.

Why Vigil over Bearing

Bearing has the strongest territory fit with Territory A — Phase 2 states "Take a bearing is the instrument's primary action" and rates Bearing as "Strongest fit" in the Territory A naming compatibility table. This is a real argument for Bearing. But three factors tip the decision to Vigil. First, Phase 2 explicitly flags that "Ask Bearing" is "slightly less idiomatic (navigation instruments don't typically precede 'ask') but falls within acceptable bounds" — the hedge is documented, not eliminated. Second, "Your Bearings" as a plural is acknowledged as "slightly weaker" than "Your Briefs" or "Your Vigils" in Phase 2. Together, they make Bearing the second-best choice: strong, clean, well-fitted to Territory A, but with three small documented frictions versus Vigil's zero.

04

Powered / Powered+ Attribution Syntax

Three-way fork from Phase 2 resolved here. This decision is binding for Phase 4. No deferral.

Phase 2 defined the three formulations under consideration: (1) "Powered by [Name]" — partner uses the post-Alfa product name; (2) "Powered by [New Name]" — partner uses a future successor name if the product name is itself in transition; (3) "Powered by Lede" — company-level endorsement when the product name is not yet established or when the partner prefers company-name attribution. The resolution maps each formulation to a specific application scenario.

Resolution: Three Scenarios
Scenario 1 — Primary formulation (post-transition, established name)
"Powered by Vigil"
When: Vigil is publicly launched and trademark-cleared in class 36 and class 42. The product name is publicly established in the market.

Application examples:
— A prime-broker integration where Vigil is the live product name: partner's platform displays "Market Intelligence Powered by Vigil"
— A data-vendor API attribution after public launch: API documentation footer reads "Powered by Vigil — Lede Professional Intelligence"
— A sell-side workflow integration launched post-Alfa transition: "Research monitoring powered by Vigil by Lede" in the partner's product documentation

This is the target-state formulation. All partner integrations migrate to it once the product name is established.
Scenario 2 — Transition-period formulation
"Powered by Lede"
When: During the Alfa-to-Vigil transition period (D3 dual-path, 60-day bench-activation clause); before trademark clearance is confirmed; or any white-label deployment where (a) the partner's brand standards require only the company name in attribution, or (b) the product name is not yet visible in the partner's market-facing materials.

Application examples:
— A white-label deployment where the partner's technology stack is not publicly attributed to any vendor by name: partner uses "Powered by Lede" in a small-type system footer
— Partner co-branded materials printed or published during the bench-activation period before Vigil is publicly launched: "Powered by Lede" holds until the transition is complete, then updated to "Powered by Vigil"
— A Powered-tier integration launched during Alfa's 60-day bench period where Vigil's name is not yet confirmed: company-level attribution until product name is confirmed and cleared

This formulation serves as the default during any naming uncertainty. It is stable, cannot be invalidated by trademark disputes, and keeps the Lede company brand in every partner context regardless of product name status.
Scenario 3 — Reserved succession formulation
"Powered by [Successor Name]"
When: Vigil is itself superseded by a future product name. Not a current application scenario.

Activation trigger: Phase 4 must document the criteria that would trigger a Vigil succession event — trademark failure post-launch, strategic repositioning, or product line consolidation. Until those criteria are defined and triggered, this formulation does not apply. All current partner integrations use Scenarios 1 or 2.
Powered+ Tier — Separate Treatment
Powered+ is not an attribution badge — it is a co-branding tier. The correct formulation for Powered+ is not "Powered by Vigil" but "[Partner Brand] + Vigil by Lede" — the full product lockup appearing alongside the partner brand at co-brand visual weight. This is a distinct format from the attribution tag used in Scenarios 1 and 2. The Powered+ tier (per D2) is defined as "co-branded partner capability" — the partner brings distribution, Vigil by Lede brings the named intelligence layer. Both names appear at equivalent visual weight in the co-brand lockup.

Powered+ application example: An institutional research platform integrates Vigil as a named capability and co-brands the offering: the platform's brand name appears alongside "Vigil by Lede" in the product UI, partner documentation, and joint marketing materials. The Step logomark appears at the standard co-brand scale (24px preferred, 16px minimum per Phase 2 spec). The partner's brand is not subordinated; both are primary within the co-branded context.

Differentiation from Powered tier: A Powered-tier integration uses "Powered by Vigil" as an attribution tag (small-type, endorsement-badge format). A Powered+ integration uses "Vigil by Lede" as a co-brand lockup at display-scale visual weight. The tier distinction is architecturally enforced by the format difference.
